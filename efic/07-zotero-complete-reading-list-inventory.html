2. Core Textbooks & Official Reference Manuals (Complete Guide)

The following six authoritative works form the foundational literature recommended by the EFIC Examination Committee:

B1 Mechanisms and Management of Pain for the Physical Therapist (2nd Edition, 2016) Kathleen A. Sluka (ed.)
IASP Press / Wolters Kluwer		 The Primary Physical Therapy Pain Bible. Covers exercise-induced hypoalgesia (EIH), central vs. peripheral mechanisms of TENS, nociceptor neurobiology, joint mobilization neurophysiology, and clinical reasoning for nociplastic pain. 01-pain-science.md, 03-principles-treatment.md
B2 Acute Pain Management: Scientific Evidence (4th Edition, 2015) Schug SA, Palmer GM, Scott DA, Halliwell R, Trinca J
ANZCA & WFSA		 Definitive 700-page evidence synthesis on acute postoperative and trauma pain. High-yield for transition from acute to chronic pain (PPSP), multimodal analgesia, early mobilization, and safety warnings for opioids/NSAIDs. 02-assessment.md, 06-master-table.md
B3 Classification of Chronic Pain: Descriptions of Chronic Pain Syndromes and Definitions of Pain Terms (2nd Ed. Rev.) Merskey H, Bogduk N (eds.)
IASP Press / Task Force on Taxonomy		 Landmark taxonomy classifying chronic pain across anatomical axes. Foundation for modern ICD-11 chronic pain codes (MG30) and standardized taxonomy definitions (allodynia, hyperalgesia, neuropathy, neuralgia). 01-iasp-taxonomy.md, 01-pain-science.md
B4 Wall and Melzack's Textbook of Pain (6th Edition, 2013) McMahon SB, Koltzenburg M, Tracey I, Turk DC (eds.)
Elsevier Saunders		 The global standard reference for comprehensive neuroanatomy, ascending tracts (spinothalamic/spinoparabrachial), descending bulbospinal gating (PAG-RVM, LC), neuro-immune glial activation, and clinical pain syndromes. 01-pain-science.md
B5 Oxford Textbook of Paediatric Pain (2013) McGrath PJ, Stevens BJ, Walker SM, Zempsky WT (eds.)
Oxford University Press		 Definitive international treatise on developmental neurobiology, neonatal nociceptive vulnerability, pediatric assessment tools across age bands (FLACC, FPS-R), and pediatric rehabilitation. 02-assessment.md, 04-special-populations.md
B6 Outcome Measures (2019) Faculty of Pain Medicine (FPM) & British Pain Society (BPS)
Royal College of Anaesthetists		 Clinical practice standard specifying core outcome datasets for chronic pain audit: Brief Pain Inventory (BPI), EQ-5D, HADS, Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire (PSEQ), TSK, and PCS. 02-assessment.md

Section One: Pain Science and Knowledge

# Citation Official Reading List Status Curriculum Focus Key Finding / ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula Study Hub Location
1 Simons, Elman, & Borsook (2014) Neurosci Biobehav Rev Official Reading List (Sec 1.3) Section 1.3 4 Brain Circuits: Sensory, Salience (Insula/ACC), Executive (PFC), Reward (VTA/NAc). Chronic pain shifts motivation to threat avoidance and causes reward blunting. 01-pain-science.md
2 Le Bars, Mouraux, & Plaghki (2017) EMC - Anesth-Réanim Official Reading List (Sec 1.3) Section 1.1 / 1.3 Peripheral Mechanisms: High-threshold nociceptor transduction; A-delta vs C fiber temporal kinetics; primary afferent neurochemistry. 01-pain-science.md
3 Mouraux, Plaghki, & Le Bars (2017) EMC - Anesth-Réanim Official Reading List (Sec 1.3) Section 1.1 / 1.3 Central Mechanisms & Controls: Ascending tracts; Diffuse Noxious Inhibitory Controls (DNIC) requiring caudal medulla (SRD); wide dynamic range (WDR) neuron properties. 01-pain-science.md
4 Malfliet et al. (2017) Eur J Pain Zotero Library (IZHXF5I8) Section 1.3 / 3.3 DLPFC Atrophy & Catastrophizing: Catastrophizing correlates with DLPFC gray matter thinning ($r = -0.66$); reversed by CBT/PNE. Resting-state DMN hyperconnectivity. 01-pain-science.md
5 Petersen et al. (2014) Pain Official Reading List (Sec 3.1) Section 1.3 / 3.1 Nocebo Hyperalgesia: Moderate-to-large effect ($g = 0.63$). Mediated by cholecystokinin (CCK) and anxiety; blocked by proglumide. 01-pain-science.md, 03-principles-treatment.md
6 Finnerup et al. (2016) Pain Zotero Library (A3VCBI48) Section 1.1 / 2.1 Neuropathic Grading: Possible (History+Distribution) $\rightarrow$ Probable (+Sensory signs) $\rightarrow$ Definite (+Objective confirmatory test). 01-pain-science.md, 02-assessment.md
7 Smart et al. (2011) Clin J Pain Official Reading List (Sec 2.1) Section 1.1 / 2.1 Mechanism Classification: Nociceptive (clear mechanical aggravating/easing) vs. Peripheral Neuropathic (dermatomal, neurodynamic+) vs. Central Sensitization (diffuse, non-mechanical). 01-pain-science.md, 02-assessment.md
8 Kosek et al. (2021) Pain Curriculum Update (2024) Section 1.1.5 Nociplastic Clinical Criteria: Pain $\ge 3\text{m}$, multifocal/widespread, cannot be explained by nociceptive/neuropathic, evoked hypersensitivity + comorbid sleep/fatigue/cognitive deficits. 01-pain-science.md

Section Two: Principles of Assessment and Measurement

# Citation Official Reading List Status Curriculum Focus Key Finding / ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula Study Hub Location
9 Fillingim, Loeser, Baron, & Edwards (2016) J Pain Official Reading List (Sec 2.1) Section 2.1 / 2.2 Assessment Domains & Mechanisms: IMMPACT 6 core domains (Pain, Function, Emotional, PGIC, Side Effects, Disposition); Quantitative Sensory Testing (QST) protocols. 02-assessment.md
10 Wang et al. (2016) CMAJ Official Reading List (Sec 2.2) Section 2.2 / 4.2 PPSP Predictors: Axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) carries highest risk ($\text{OR} = 2.41$); younger age ($\text{OR} = 1.36$); acute post-op pain ($\text{OR} = 1.16$). Chemotherapy has no association. 02-assessment.md
11 Verbunt, Huijnen, & Seelen (2012) Clin J Pain Official Reading List (Sec 2.2) Section 2.2 / 3.4 Objective Accelerometry: Self-report correlates poorly with actual movement. Valid physical activity measurement requires 4–7 consecutive days of accelerometry. 02-assessment.md
12 Tyson & Brown (2014) Clin Rehabil Official Reading List (Sec 2.2) Section 2.1 / 2.2 Neurological Measurement: NRS superior in cognitively intact; Right-hemisphere neglect $\rightarrow$ use VRS; Left-hemisphere aphasia $\rightarrow$ use NRS/VAS. 02-assessment.md
13 Gordon (2015) Curr Opin Anaesthesiol Official Reading List (Sec 2.2) Section 2.1 / 2.2 Dynamic Acute Assessment: Move beyond resting 0–10 NRS; assess pain on movement/coughing. MCID is $\ge 2$ points or $\ge 30\%$ reduction. 02-assessment.md
14 Hamill et al. (2014) Pain Zotero Library (ZEESGM3K) Section 2.1 / 4.3 Pediatric Body Maps: Valid and reliable in children aged $\ge 4\text{ to }5\text{ years}$ for pain localization (measures location, NOT intensity). 02-assessment.md, 04-special-populations.md
15 Hatfield & Ely (2015) Biol Res Nurs Zotero Library (KXER4YTR) Section 2.1 / 4.3 Infant Acute Scales: Validated behavioral scales (FLACC, NIPS, PIPP); behavioral observation > autonomic signs. Untreated NICU pain alters future sensitivity. 02-assessment.md, 04-special-populations.md

Section Three: Principles of Treatment

# Citation Official Reading List Status Curriculum Focus Key Finding / ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula Study Hub Location
16 Testa & Rossettini (2016) Man Ther Official Reading List (Sec 3.1) Section 3.2 / 3.3 5 Contextual Factors in PT: PT traits, Patient traits, Alliance, Treatment rituals, Clinic environment. Harnessing placebo ethically ($g \approx 0.5\text{–}0.7$). 03-principles-treatment.md
17 Geneen et al. (2015) Syst Rev Official Reading List (Sec 3.2) Section 3.4 Pain Education Review: Improves pain knowledge ($\text{SMD} = 0.54$) and reduces catastrophizing ($\text{SMD} = -0.37$); requires active exercise to alter disability. 03-principles-treatment.md
18 Engers et al. (2008) Cochrane Review Official Reading List (Sec 3.2) Section 3.4 Patient Education Timing: Brief education effective for acute LBP; ineffective for chronic LBP (requires intensive biopsychosocial education). 03-principles-treatment.md
19 Moseley & Butler (2015) J Pain Official Reading List (Sec 3.2) Section 3.4 Explaining Pain (PNE): Pain is a marker of perceived threat/danger. PNE reduces catastrophizing and healthcare use; pair with active movement. 03-principles-treatment.md
20 Nicholas et al. (2011) Phys Ther Official Reading List (Sec 3.3) Section 3.5 The Flags Framework: Red (biomedical), Orange (psychiatric), Yellow (psychological/beliefs), Blue (workplace), Black (systemic/insurance). 03-principles-treatment.md
21 Linton & Shaw (2011) Phys Ther Official Reading List (Sec 3.3) Section 3.5 Psychological Factors: Fear-avoidance, catastrophizing, and depression alter pain processing and drive long-term disability. 03-principles-treatment.md
22 Eccleston et al. (2014) Cochrane Review Official Reading List (Sec 3.3) Section 3.5 / 3.7 Internet-Delivered CBT: Moderate reduction on disability ($\text{SMD} = -0.50$); small on pain ($\text{SMD} = -0.37$). Therapist-guided >> Unguided. 03-principles-treatment.md
23 Macedo et al. (2010) Phys Ther Official Reading List (Sec 3.3) Section 3.5 Graded Activity vs. Graded Exposure: Activity = operant quota-based pacing; Exposure = in-vivo violation of threatening expectations (PHODA). 03-principles-treatment.md
24 Linton, Flink, & Vlaeyen (2018) Phys Ther Official Reading List (Sec 3.3) Section 3.5 Psychological Etiology: Operant vs respondent conditioning; avoidance acts as a safety behavior maintaining threat expectations. 03-principles-treatment.md
25 Veehof et al. (2016) Cogn Behav Ther Zotero Library / Exam Section 3.5 ACT & Mindfulness: Low-to-high quality; Moderate effect on pain interference ($\text{SMD} = 0.62$) & anxiety ($\text{SMD} = 0.51$); Small on pain intensity. 03-principles-treatment.md
26 Henschke et al. (2010) Cochrane Review Zotero Library (SSUFJJ6D) Section 3.5 Behavioral Therapy for CLBP: Behavioral therapy is superior to waitlist control for short-term pain relief and functional improvement. 03-principles-treatment.md
27 Flor (2014) Phys Ther Zotero Library (PCFVJ3UB) Section 3.5 CBT Neurophysiology: Restores normal somatosensory receptive fields in $S1$ and dampens hyperactive limbic salience circuits (ACC/insula). 03-principles-treatment.md
28 Geneen et al. (2017) Cochrane Overview Official Reading List (Sec 3.4) Section 3.6 Exercise in Chronic Pain: Physical activity consistently reduces pain and disability across all chronic pain states; minimal adverse events. 03-principles-treatment.md
29 Lima, Abner, & Sluka (2017) J Physiol Official Reading List (Sec 3.4) Section 3.6 Exercise Mechanisms (Analgesia vs Hyperalgesia): Analgesia via RVM opioids, $5\text{-HT}_1A$, and $\alpha_2$-receptors. Hyperalgesia in sensitized patients due to impaired CPM and eccentric loading. 03-principles-treatment.md
30 Sluka, Frey-Law, & Hoeger Bement (2018) Pain Official Reading List (Sec 3.4) Section 3.6 Exercise Translation: Aerobic ($60\text{–}75\%$ HRR) and isometric ($20\text{–}25\%$ MVC) induce EIH; chronic pain requires low initial dosing to avoid flare-ups. 03-principles-treatment.md
31 Oliveira et al. (2016) Arthritis Care Res Official Reading List (Sec 3.4) Section 3.6 Objectively Measured PA: Exercise interventions alone do not increase daily step counts without explicit behavioral activity coaching. 03-principles-treatment.md
32 Oliveira et al. (2018) Arch Phys Med Rehabil Official Reading List (Sec 3.4) Section 3.6 / 3.7 Electronic Feedback Limits: Wearables and step counters alone fail to reduce pain or disability without clinician-led behavioral coaching. 03-principles-treatment.md
33 Booth et al. (2017) Musculoskeletal Care Official Reading List (Sec 3.4) Section 3.6 Biopsychosocial Exercise: Impaired EIH requires combining exercise dosing with biopsychosocial reconceptualization. 03-principles-treatment.md
34 Bidonde et al. (2017) Cochrane Review Zotero Library (RWXCHRLL) Section 3.6 Aerobic Exercise in Fibromyalgia: Moderate-quality evidence for improved HRQoL ($\text{MD} = -5.6/100$), physical function, and pain ($\text{MD} = -8.8/100$). 03-principles-treatment.md
35 Searle et al. (2015) Clin Rehabil Zotero Library (R5DHUWHE) Section 3.6 Exercise in CLBP: Motor control ($\text{SMD} = -0.49$) and resistance training ($\text{SMD} = -0.45$) show small-to-moderate effects; 12m outcomes are equivalent across modalities. 03-principles-treatment.md
36 Gatchel et al. (2014) Am Psychol Official Reading List (Sec 3.5) Section 3.9 / 5.1 Interdisciplinary vs. Multidisciplinary: Interdisciplinary (integrated team with regular case conferences & shared goals) delivers superior RTW and cost savings. 03-principles-treatment.md, 05-interprofessional-teamwork.md
37 Oesch et al. (2010) J Rehabil Med Official Reading List (Sec 3.6) Section 3.8 Work Disability Meta-Analysis: High-intensity conditioning and quota-based exercise reduce sick leave in subacute/chronic workers. 03-principles-treatment.md
38 Schaafsma et al. (2013) Cochrane Review Official Reading List (Sec 3.6) Section 3.8 Work Conditioning: Reduces sick leave by an average of 45 fewer days at 12 months when work simulation and workplace visits are included. 03-principles-treatment.md
39 Kamper et al. (2015) Cochrane / BMJ Zotero Library (SGUZYJYR) Section 3.8 / 3.9 Multidisciplinary Biopsychosocial Rehab: Superior to physical therapy alone at $>12\text{ months}$; increases odds of Return to Work ($\mathbf{\text{OR} = 1.87}$). 03-principles-treatment.md
40 Bowering et al. (2013) J Pain Official Reading List (Sec 3.7) Section 3.10 Graded Motor Imagery: Efficacy requires strict sequence: (1) Laterality $\rightarrow$ (2) Imagined movements $\rightarrow$ (3) Mirror therapy. 03-principles-treatment.md
41 Johnson & Jones (2017) Pain Manag Official Reading List (Sec 3.7) Section 3.10 TENS Current Evidence: Safe, self-managed neuromodulatory adjunct; RCT evidence remains low-to-moderate quality due to trial design challenges. 03-principles-treatment.md
42 Jensen, Day, & Miró (2014) Nat Rev Neurol Official Reading List (Sec 3.7) Section 3.10 Neuromodulatory Treatments: Mechanisms of tDCS, rTMS, and neurofeedback; optimal when combined with active motor retraining. 03-principles-treatment.md
43 Ebadi et al. (2014) Cochrane Review Zotero Library (YDK2GRSE) Section 3.10 Ultrasound in CLBP: No evidence of benefit over sham ($\text{MD} = 2.16\text{ mm}$ on 100mm VAS). Therapeutic ultrasound is NOT recommended. 03-principles-treatment.md
44 Rubinstein et al. (2011) Cochrane / Spine Zotero Library (TY57JCXM) Section 3.10 Spinal Manipulation: Small short-term pain relief; equivalent to exercise and GP care, NOT clinically superior. 03-principles-treatment.md
45 Franke, Franke, & Fryer (2014) BMC MSK Official Reading List (Sec 3.9) Section 3.10 Osteopathy for LBP: Small-to-moderate short-term pain relief ($\text{MD} = -12.91\text{ mm}$) and functional improvement over sham. 03-principles-treatment.md
46 Patti et al. (2015) Medicine Official Reading List (Sec 3.9) Section 3.10 Pilates in CLBP: Small-to-moderate improvements in pain and disability; equivalent to other active exercise forms. 03-principles-treatment.md
47 Wieland et al. (2017) Cochrane Review Official Reading List (Sec 3.9) Section 3.10 Yoga in CLBP: Small-to-moderate improvements in back-specific function and pain; clinically equivalent to standard physical therapy. 03-principles-treatment.md
48 Crawford et al. (2014) Pain Med Official Reading List (Sec 3.9) Section 3.10 Active Self-Care CAM: Mind-body CAM therapies (yoga, tai chi) empower active patient self-efficacy over passive dependence. 03-principles-treatment.md

53 Makris et al. (2014) JAMA Official Reading List (Sec 4.4) Section 4.3 Geriatric Clinical Review: High risk of multimorbidity/polypharmacy; systemic NSAIDs (GI/renal risk) and TCAs (falls/arrhythmias) should be avoided; exercise first-line. 04-special-populations.md
54 Herr (2011) J Pain Official Reading List (Sec 4.4) Section 4.3 Geriatric Assessment Hierarchy: (1) Self-report $\rightarrow$ (2) Underlying pathology $\rightarrow$ (3) Observable pain behaviors $\rightarrow$ (4) Caregiver proxy $\rightarrow$ (5) Empirical analgesic trial. 02-assessment.md, 04-special-populations.md
55 Lichtner et al. (2014) BMC Geriatr Zotero Library (7HQW9HSG) Section 4.3 Dementia Assessment Meta-Review: Evaluated 28 tools; NO SINGLE GOLD STANDARD; behavioral observation during guided movement is vastly superior to physiological signs. 02-assessment.md, 04-special-populations.md
56 Karp et al. (2008) Br J Anaesth Zotero Library (BFI3QMM9) Section 4.3 Older Adult Neurobiology: C-fiber loss, reduced CPM, altered hepatic/renal clearance; heightened fall risk with opioids/TCAs. 04-special-populations.md
57 Harrison et al. (2019) J Clin Med Official Reading List (Sec 4.5) Section 4.3 Pediatric Pain Rehabilitation: Function-first; EXPANSE graded exposure in vivo; reducing parental solicitousness; Return to School (RTS) protocol. 04-special-populations.md
58 Fisher et al. (2019) Cochrane Review Official Reading List (Sec 4.5) Section 4.3 / 3.7 Pediatric Remotely Delivered CBT: Internet CBT significantly reduces headache frequency and chronic pain ($\text{OR} = 2.47\text{ for }\ge 50\%\text{ pain reduction}$). 04-special-populations.md
59 Fisher et al. (2018) Cochrane Review Official Reading List (Sec 4.5) Section 4.3 Pediatric Face-to-Face CBT: Significant pain reduction in headache and abdominal pain; improvements in disability and anxiety. 04-special-populations.md
60 Eccleston et al. (2015) Cochrane Review Zotero Library (9DIZC3C4) Section 4.3 Parent Interventions in Pediatric Chronic Illness: Targeting parents reduces parental distress ($\text{SMD} = -0.27$) and solicitousness, directly improving child school attendance. 04-special-populations.md
