EFIC Core Curriculum & Reading List Syllabus Mapping

This document aligns the European Pain Federation (EFIC®) Core Curriculum for the European Diploma in Pain Physiotherapy with the exact required and recommended literature.

Section 1: Pain Science and Knowledge

1.1 Pain Mechanisms

  • Objectives:
    • 1.1.1 Outline mechanisms of acute pain, inflammation, neuropathic pain, and functional pain syndromes.
    • 1.1.2 Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of underlying physiology and biology of acute and chronic pain.
    • 1.1.3 Discuss the distinction between nociception and pain.
    • 1.1.4 Current theories of pain: Revised Gate-Control Theory, Neuromatrix Theory (Melzack), Fear-Avoidance and Endurance Model, Onion-Skin Model (Loeser), Mature Organism Model (Gifford), Mechanism-Based Approach.
    • 1.1.5 Describe: Transduction, transmission, modulation in nociceptive pathways; Ascending and descending pathways; Peripheral vs Central Sensitization; Primary vs Secondary Hyperalgesia; Neurobiology (Spinal inhibition/facilitation, Somatic/visceral nerves, Opioidergic/GABAergic PAG-RVM pathway, Adrenergic Locus Coeruleus pathway, Cortical top-down processing, Somatosensory system, Autonomic system); Neuronal-glial interactions.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Textbooks on Pain: Sluka (ed.) Mechanisms and Management of Pain for the Physical Therapist; Melzack & Wall’s Textbook of Pain (6th ed).

1.2 IASP Definition of Pain and Taxonomy

  • Objectives:
    • 1.2.1 Describe key historical milestones: Cartesian Dualism, Germ-theory (Pasteur/Koch), Cell-theory (Virchow), Specificity vs Gate Control, Sensitization-theory.
    • 1.2.2 Define common pain phenomena according to IASP taxonomy (IASP 2020 revision: "An unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with, or resembling that associated with, actual or potential tissue damage").
    • 1.2.3 Define clinical pain terminology: Acute, chronic (>3 months), neuropathic, spontaneous, breakthrough pain.
    • 1.2.4 Define Pain-Oriented Sensory Testing (POST): Sensory threshold, pain threshold, pain tolerance, punctate mechanical allodynia, dynamic and static mechanical allodynia, cold/warmth allodynia.
    • 1.2.5 Discuss sensory testing related to phenotypic changes in peripheral neurons (heat/cold/mechanical hyperalgesia).
  • Reading List Sources:
    • IASP Taxonomy & Classification of Chronic Pain: Merskey & Bogduk (1994/2012); IASP online taxonomy (iasp-pain.org/Taxonomy).

1.3 The Complex and Multidimensional Nature of Pain

  • Objectives:
    • 1.3.1 Discuss evolution of conceptual models (Biopsychosocial model); Brain changes in chronic pain (cognition, memory, mood); Behavioral/cognitive mechanisms (fear-avoidance, catastrophizing, operant/classical conditioning); Neurophysiological overlap with co-morbidities (stress, sleep, anxiety, depression); Placebo and Nocebo mechanisms; Genetics and epigenetics.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Malfliet A, Coppieters I, Van Wilgen P, et al. (2017). Brain changes associated with cognitive and emotional factors in chronic pain: A systematic review. Eur J Pain, 21(5):769-786.
    • Simons LE, Elman I, Borsook D. (2014). Psychological processing in chronic pain: a neural systems approach. Neurosci Biobehav Rev, 39:61-78.

Section 2: Principles of Assessment and Measurement

2.1 Assessment

  • Objectives:
    • 2.1.1 Comprehensive biopsychosocial assessment across the life span.
    • 2.1.2 Rationale behind basic biopsychosocial assessments.
    • 2.1.3 Cultural, societal, economic, institutional influences on pain assessment.
    • 2.1.4 Patient and provider factors (attitudes, beliefs, health literacy, affective/cognitive/behavioral responses).
    • 2.1.5 Person-centred approach (biological, functional, psychological, work, social).
    • 2.1.6 Rationale for basic investigations (red flags, serious pathology, neuropathies, musculoskeletal).
    • 2.1.7 Specialist assessment and appropriate multidisciplinary referral.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • WHO ICF: International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health.
    • Finnerup NB, Haroutounian S, Kamerman P, Baron R, Bennett DL, et al. (2016). Neuropathic pain: an updated grading system for research and clinical practice. Pain, 157(8):1599-606.
    • Smart KM, Blake C, Doody C. (2011). The Discriminative validity of "nociceptive," "peripheral neuropathic," and "central sensitization" as mechanisms-based classifications of musculoskeletal pain. Clin J Pain, 27(8):655-63.

2.2 Outcome Measures & Measurement Tools

  • Objectives:
    • 2.2.1 Critical selection of valid/reliable physical and psychological outcome measures across ICF domains.
    • 2.2.2 Physical assessment (activity levels, daily function, functional capacity, sleep).
    • 2.2.3 Validated tools for monitoring across lifespan and special populations (pediatric, dementia, cognitive impairment, cultural/linguistic diversity).
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Wang L, Guyatt GH, Kennedy SA, et al. (2016). Predictors of persistent pain after breast cancer surgery: a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies. CMAJ, 188:E352-e361.
    • Verbunt JA, Huijnen IP, Seelen HA. (2012). Assessment of physical activity by movement registration systems in chronic pain: methodological considerations. Clin J Pain, 28(6):496-504.
    • Tyson SF, Brown P. (2014). How to measure pain in neurological conditions? A systematic review of psychometric properties and clinical utility of measurement tools. Clin Rehabil, 28(7):669-86.
    • Gordon DB. (2015). Acute pain assessment tools: let us move beyond simple pain ratings. Curr Opin Anaesthesiol, 28(5):565-9.

Section 3: Principles of Treatment

3.1 Communication & Therapeutic Alliance

  • Objectives: 3.1.1 Individualized evidence-based management plan; 3.1.2 Multimodal vs Multidisciplinary triage; 3.1.3 Shared decision-making & health literacy.
  • Reading List Source:
    • Petersen GL, Finnerup NB, Colloca L, Amanzio M, Price DD, Jensen TS, Vase L. (2014). The magnitude of nocebo effects in pain: a meta-analysis. Pain, 155(8):1426-34.

3.2 Pain Neuroscience Education (PNE)

  • Objectives: 3.2.1 Teaching pain neurophysiology; 3.2.2 Service delivery modes (1-on-1, group, online); 3.2.3 Self-management coaching; 3.2.4 Variables influencing learning; 3.2.5 Provider attitudes/beliefs.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Geneen LJ, Martin DJ, Adams N, et al. (2015). Effects of education to facilitate knowledge about chronic pain for adults: a systematic review with meta-analysis. Syst Rev, 4:132.
    • Engers A, Jellema P, Wensing M, van der Windt DA, Grol R, van Tulder MW. (2008). Individual patient education for low back pain. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (1).
    • Moseley GL, Butler DS. (2015). Fifteen Years of Explaining Pain: The Past, Present, and Future. J Pain, 16(9):807-13.

3.3 Behavioural Therapies & Psychological Risk Factors

  • Objectives: CBT, ACT, MBSR, relaxation/biofeedback, autogenic training, graded exposure to feared movements, yellow flags identification.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Persson AL, Veenhuizen H, et al. (2008). Relaxation as treatment for chronic musculoskeletal pain: a systematic review. Phys Ther Rev, 13(5):355-365.
    • O'Keeffe M, Purtill H, Kennedy N, Conneely M, et al. (2016). Comparative Effectiveness of Conservative Interventions for Nonspecific Chronic Spinal Pain: Physical, Behavioral/Psychologically Informed, or Combined? J Pain, 17(7):755-74.
    • Nicholas MK, Linton SJ, Watson PJ, Main CJ. (2011). Early identification and management of psychological risk factors ("yellow flags") in patients with low back pain: a reappraisal. Phys Ther, 91(5):737-53.
    • Linton SJ, Shaw WS. (2011). Impact of psychological factors in the experience of pain. Phys Ther, 91(5):700-11.
    • Eccleston C, Fisher E, Craig L, Duggan GB, et al. (2014). Psychological therapies (Internet-delivered) for the management of chronic pain in adults. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (2).
    • Flor H. (2014). Psychological pain interventions and neurophysiology: implications for a mechanism-based approach. Am Psychol, 69(2):188-96.
    • Henschke N, Ostelo RW, van Tulder MW, Vlaeyen JW, Morley S, Assendelft WJ, Main CJ. (2010). Behavioural treatment for chronic low-back pain. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (7).
    • Macedo LG, Smeets RJ, Maher CG, Latimer J, McAuley JH. (2010). Graded activity and graded exposure for persistent nonspecific low back pain: a systematic review. Phys Ther, 90(6):860-79.

3.4 Exercise & Physical Activity

  • Objectives: Evidence-based exercise prescription, adaptation for co-morbidities, biopsychosocial exercise delivery, pacing, graded activity.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Geneen LJ, Moore RA, Clarke C, Martin D, et al. (2017). Physical activity and exercise for chronic pain in adults: an overview of Cochrane Reviews. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 4.
    • Bidonde J, Busch AJ, Schachter CL, et al. (2017). Aerobic exercise training for adults with fibromyalgia. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 6.
    • Searle A, Spink M, et al. (2015). Exercise interventions for the treatment of chronic low back pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Clin Rehabil, 29(12):1155-67.
    • Oliveira CB, Franco MR, Maher CG, et al. (2016). Physical Activity Interventions for Increasing Objectively Measured Physical Activity Levels in Patients With Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain. Arthritis Care Res, 68(12):1832-1842.
    • Booth J, Moseley GL, Schiltenwolf M, Cashin A, Davies M, Hübscher M. (2017). Exercise for chronic musculoskeletal pain: A biopsychosocial approach. Musculoskelet Care, 15(4):413-421.

3.5 Interdisciplinary & Medical Referral

  • Objectives: Interdisciplinary team collaboration, procedural modalities knowledge, pharmacological classes (NSAIDs, opioids, antidepressants, anticonvulsants, capsaicin, cannabinoids, corticosteroids) and their limitations.
  • Reading List Source:
    • Gatchel RJ, McGeary DD, McGeary CA, Lippe B. (2014). Interdisciplinary chronic pain management: past, present, and future. Am Psychol, 69(2):119-30.

3.6 Work & Occupational Health

  • Objectives: Work factors, work loss predictors, return-to-work (RTW) strategies, workplace adaptations.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Oesch P, Kool J, et al. (2010). Effectiveness of exercise on work disability in patients with non-acute non-specific low back pain: Systematic review & meta-analysis. J Rehabil Med, 42(3):193-205.
    • Schaafsma F, Schonstein E, et al. (2010/2013). Physical conditioning programs for improving work outcomes in workers with back pain. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (1)/(8).

3.7 Physical & Neuromodulatory Treatment Modalities

  • Objectives: Indications, efficacy, and evidence for GMI, TENS, Ultrasound, Spinal Manipulation.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Bowering KJ, O'Connell NE, Tabor A, et al. (2013). The effects of graded motor imagery and its components on chronic pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Pain, 14(1):3-13.
    • Johnson MI, Mulvey MR, et al. (2015). Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) for phantom pain and stump pain following amputation in adults. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 8.
    • Ebadi S, Henschke N, Nakhostin Ansari N, Fallah E, van Tulder MW. (2014). Therapeutic ultrasound for chronic low-back pain. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (3).
    • Rubinstein SM, van Middelkoop M, et al. (2011). Spinal manipulative therapy for chronic low-back pain: an update of a Cochrane review. Spine, 36(13):E825-46.

3.8 Treatment Effects & Rehabilitation

  • Objectives: Multidisciplinary biopsychosocial rehab, functional restoration, adherence factors, arthroplasty rehab.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Kamper SJ, Apeldoorn AT, et al. (2015). Multidisciplinary biopsychosocial rehabilitation for chronic low back pain: Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ, 350:h444.
    • Richards MC, Ford JJ, Slater SL, et al. (2013). The effectiveness of physiotherapy functional restoration for post-acute low back pain: a systematic review. Man Ther, 18(1):4-25.
    • Thompson EL, Broadbent J, Bertino MD, Staiger PK. (2016). Do Pain-related Beliefs Influence Adherence to Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation?: A Systematic Review. Clin J Pain, 32(2):164-78.
    • Khan F, Ng L, et al. (2008). Multidisciplinary rehabilitation programmes following joint replacement at the hip and knee in chronic arthropathy. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (2).

3.9 Allied & Complementary Therapies

  • Objectives: Evidence base and safety of Yoga, Osteopathy, Pilates, MBSR, and Acupuncture for chronic pain.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Cramer H, Lauche R, Haller H, Dobos G. (2013). A systematic review and meta-analysis of yoga for low back pain. Clin J Pain, 29(5):450-60.
    • Franke H, Franke JD, Fryer G. (2014). Osteopathic manipulative treatment for nonspecific low back pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Musculoskelet Disord, 15:286.
    • Patti A, Bianco A, Paoli A, et al. (2015). Effects of Pilates exercise programs in people with chronic low back pain: a systematic review. Medicine (Baltimore), 94(4).
    • Cramer H, Haller H, Lauche R, Dobos G. (2012). Mindfulness-based stress reduction for low back pain. A systematic review. BMC Complement Altern Med, 12:162.
    • Standaert CJ, Friedly J, Erwin MW, et al. (2011). Comparative effectiveness of exercise, acupuncture, and spinal manipulation for low back pain. Spine, 36(21 Suppl):S120-30.

Section 4: Pain Subgroups / Special Patient Populations

4.1 Specific Pain Conditions & CRPS

  • Reading List Sources:
    • Smart KM, Wand BM, O'Connell NE. (2016). Physiotherapy for pain and disability in adults with complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) types I and II. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 2:CD010853.
    • IASP Factsheets (Neuropathic, Musculoskeletal, Visceral, CRPS).

4.2 & 4.4 Older Adults & Dementia

  • Objectives: Altered pathophysiology, cognitive co-morbidities, observational pain tools, analgesic risks.
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Lichtner V, Dowding D, Esterhuizen P, et al. (2014). Pain assessment for people with dementia: a systematic review of systematic reviews of pain assessment tools. BMC Geriatr, 14:138.

    • Herr K. (2011). Pain assessment strategies in older patients. J Pain, 12(3 Suppl 1):S3-S13.

4.5 Infants, Children, and Adolescents

  • Objectives: Long-term neurophysiological consequences of early pain, observational vs self-report, parent interventions, remote/digital CBT, RTS/SIS (Return to School / Stay in School).
  • Reading List Sources:
    • Textbook: McGrath PJ (ed.) Oxford Textbook of Paediatric Pain (2014).
    • Fisher E, Law E, et al. (2015). Psychological therapies (remotely delivered) for the management of chronic and recurrent pain in children and adolescents. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (3).
    • Eccleston C, Fisher E, Law E, Bartlett J, Palermo TM. (2015). Psychological interventions for parents of children and adolescents with chronic illness. Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (4).
    • Hatfield LA, Ely EA. (2015). Measurement of acute pain in infants: a review of behavioral and physiological variables. Biol Res Nurs, 17(1):100-11.
    • Hamill JK, Lyndon M, Liley A, Hill AG. (2014). Where it hurts: a systematic review of pain-location tools for children. Pain, 155(5):851-8.
