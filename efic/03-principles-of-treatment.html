Section 3: Principles of Treatment — EFIC Ingest & Study Guide

This module covers the core clinical interventions, Cochrane review evidence bases, mechanism-based therapies, and multidisciplinary strategies for Section Three of the EFIC Core Curriculum.

1. Communication & Education (3.1 & 3.2)

📄 Paper 7: Petersen et al. (2014) — Nocebo Hyperalgesia Meta-Analysis

Citation: Petersen GL, Finnerup NB, Colloca L, Amanzio M, Price DD, Jensen TS, Vase L. The magnitude of nocebo effects in pain: a meta-analysis. Pain. 2014 Aug;155(8):1426-34. DOI: 10.1016/j.pain.2014.04.016. - Key Findings: - Nocebo hyperalgesia has a moderate-to-large effect size (Hedges' $g = 0.63$). - Negative verbal suggestions combined with conditioning produce the strongest nocebo hyperalgesia effects ($g = 0.77$), but verbal suggestions alone also produce significant hyperalgesia ($g = 0.52$). - Physiological Mechanism: Mediated by the cholecystokinin (CCK) system and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis activation; can be blocked by CCK antagonists (proglumide). - Clinical Rule for MCQ: Clinician language, warnings about catastrophic anatomy, and diagnostic framing can directly induce real, neurobiologically mediated pain increases.

📄 Paper 8: Moseley & Butler (2015) — 15 Years of Explaining Pain

Citation: Moseley GL, Butler DS. Fifteen Years of Explaining Pain: The Past, Present, and Future. J Pain. 2015 Sep;16(9):807-13. DOI: 10.1016/j.jpain.2015.05.005. - Key Concepts: - Pain Neuroscience Education (PNE) shifts the conceptualization of pain from a marker of tissue damage to a marker of perceived danger/threat. - PNE delivers measurable reductions in pain catastrophizing, kinesiophobia, pain-related disability, and healthcare utilization. - PNE is most effective when paired with active movement and functional rehabilitation rather than as a standalone passive lecture.

📄 Paper 9: Geneen et al. (2015) & Engers et al. (2008) — Patient Education

  • Geneen et al. (2015): Systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrating that structured pain education significantly improves pain knowledge and short-term pain self-efficacy, but requires active behavioral coaching for long-term behavioral change.
  • Engers et al. (2008) Cochrane Review on LBP Education:
    • In acute low back pain, brief patient reassurance and advice to stay active (e.g. The Back Book) reduces sick leave and healthcare consultations compared to usual care.
    • In chronic low back pain, intensive 1-on-1 education or multidisciplinary education is needed; brief generic advice is insufficient.

2. Behavioural Therapies & Psychological Risk Factors (3.3)

📄 Paper 10: Nicholas et al. (2011) — The "Decade of the Flags"

Citation: Nicholas MK, Linton SJ, Watson PJ, Main CJ; "Decade of the Flags" Working Group. Early identification and management of psychological risk factors ("yellow flags") in patients with low back pain: a reappraisal. Phys Ther. 2011 May;91(5):737-53. DOI: 10.2522/ptj.20100224. - The Flags Hierarchy (Essential for MCQ): - Red Flags: Biomedical indicators of serious underlying pathology (fracture, malignancy, infection, cauda equina syndrome). - Orange Flags: Psychiatric comorbidities (clinical depression, severe anxiety, personality disorder, active psychosis). - Yellow Flags: Psychological factors and cognitive appraisals (catastrophizing, fear-avoidance beliefs, passive coping, unhelpful beliefs about hurt vs harm). - Blue Flags: Social and workplace perceptions (perceived lack of supervisor support, high job demand, low job control, fear of re-injury at work). - Black Flags: Systemic and institutional barriers (compensation/litigation claims, rigid sickness certification policies, delayed insurance approvals).

📄 Paper 11: Macedo et al. (2010) — Graded Activity vs. Graded Exposure

Citation: Macedo LG, Smeets RJ, Maher CG, Latimer J, McAuley JH. Graded activity and graded exposure for persistent nonspecific low back pain: a systematic review. Phys Ther. 2010 Jun;90(6):860-79. DOI: 10.2522/ptj.20090303. - Graded Activity (Operant Conditioning): - Quota-based, time-contingent exercise progression designed to extinguish pain behaviors and positively reinforce functional activity regardless of pain level. - Graded Exposure (Cognitive-Behavioral / Expectation Violation): - Uses the PHODA (Photographs of Daily Activities) or Fear Hierarchy to identify specific feared movements $\rightarrow$ systematically confronts patients with feared movements to disconfirm threatening catastrophic expectations. - Evidence Comparison: Both approaches are effective; Graded Exposure achieves slightly greater reductions in catastrophizing and fear-avoidance in highly fearful patients.

📄 Paper 12: Flor (2014) & Henschke et al. (2010) — CBT & Neurophysiology

  • Flor (2014): Mechanism-based CBT modifies cortical reorganization, restores normal somatosensory receptive fields in $S1$, and dampens hyperactivity in the limbic salience network (ACC/insula).
  • Henschke et al. (2010) Cochrane Review: Behavioral treatment (operant, cognitive, respondent) is more effective than wait-list control for short-term pain relief and functional improvement in chronic LBP.

📄 Paper 12b: Veehof et al. (2016) — Acceptance & Mindfulness-Based Interventions

Citation: Veehof MM, Oskam M, Schreurs KM, Bohlmeijer ET. Acceptance- and mindfulness-based interventions for the treatment of chronic pain: a meta-analytic review. Cogn Behav Ther. 2016;45(1):5-31. DOI: 10.1080/16506073.2015.1098724. - Evidence Quality: Low-to-high quality across 25 RCTs ($N=1,285$). - Effect Sizes by Outcome: - Moderate effect: Specifically on pain interference ($\text{SMD} = 0.62$, growing to large at follow-up) and anxiety ($\text{SMD} = 0.51$). - Small effect: On pain intensity ($\text{SMD} = 0.37$), depression ($\text{SMD} = 0.35$), and disability ($\text{SMD} = 0.31$). - Subgroups: ACT showed higher effect sizes on depression and anxiety than MBSR or MBCT. - ⚡ MCQ Takeaway: Evidence is low-to-high quality showing moderate effects on pain interference and anxiety, but only small effects on raw pain intensity.

3. Exercise & Physical Activity (3.4)

📄 Paper 13: Geneen et al. (2017) — Cochrane Overview of Exercise in Chronic Pain

Citation: Geneen LJ, Moore RA, Clarke C, Martin D, Colvin LA, Smith BH. Physical activity and exercise for chronic pain in adults: an overview of Cochrane Reviews. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2017 Apr 24;4(4):CD011279. - Conclusions: - Physical activity and exercise produce small-to-moderate reductions in pain severity and improved physical function across diverse chronic pain conditions (osteoarthritis, LBP, fibromyalgia, rheumatoid arthritis, neck pain). - Adverse events are predominantly mild and transient (soreness). Exercise is safe and recommended as a first-line core intervention.

📄 Paper 14: Bidonde et al. (2017) — Aerobic Exercise for Fibromyalgia

Citation: Bidonde J, Busch AJ, Schachter CL, et al. Aerobic exercise training for adults with fibromyalgia. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2017 Jun 21;6(6):CD009747. - Evidence Level: Moderate-quality evidence that aerobic exercise training improves health-related quality of life (HRQoL), reduces pain, and improves physical function in fibromyalgia compared to control. - Prescription Principle: Low-to-moderate intensity aerobic exercise (walking, cycling, aquatic exercise) with gradual progression to avoid symptom exacerbation.

📄 Paper 15: Searle et al. (2015) — Exercise Modalities in Chronic LBP

Citation: Searle A, Spink M, Ho A, Chuter V. Exercise interventions for the treatment of chronic low back pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. Clin Rehabil. 2015;29(12):1155-67. - Comparative Findings: - Core / motor control exercises, resistance training, and aerobic exercise are all effective. - Motor control exercise and strength/resistance training showed slightly higher effect sizes for reducing pain and disability compared to other exercise forms.

4. Modalities & Interventions Evidence (3.6 & 3.7)

📄 Paper 16: Bowering et al. (2013) — Graded Motor Imagery (GMI)

Citation: Bowering KJ, O'Connell NE, Tabor A, Catley MJ, Leake HB, Moseley GL. The effects of graded motor imagery and its components on chronic pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Pain. 2013 Jan;14(1):3-13. - The 3-Stage GMI Protocol: 1. Left/Right Judgement (Laterality Recognition): Restoring cortical body representations without activating primary motor cortex. 2. Motor Imagery (Imagined Movement): Mentally rehearsing movement activating premotor and supplementary motor areas. 3. Mirror Therapy: Visual feedback of unaffected limb moving to trick the sensory-motor cortex into perceiving pain-free motion. - Evidence: Substantial pain relief in CRPS Type I and Phantom Limb Pain when performed sequentially. Disordered sequence reduces efficacy.

📄 Paper 17: Johnson et al. (2015) — TENS in Phantom Limb Pain

Citation: Johnson MI, Mulvey MR, Bagnall AM. Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) for phantom pain and stump pain following amputation in adults. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2015 Aug 18;(8):CD007264. - Cochrane Conclusion: Evidence from RCTs is currently inconclusive due to lack of high-quality randomized trials, though clinical consensus and low-risk profile justify a clinical trial in individual patients.

📄 Paper 18: Ebadi et al. (2014) — Therapeutic Ultrasound for Chronic LBP

Citation: Ebadi S, Henschke N, Nakhostin Ansari N, Fallah E, van Tulder MW. Therapeutic ultrasound for chronic low-back pain. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2014 Mar 14;(3):CD009169. - Cochrane Conclusion (High-Yield Negative Result): - There is no high-quality evidence that therapeutic ultrasound is effective for chronic low back pain. Therapeutic ultrasound is NOT recommended as an active treatment for chronic LBP.

📄 Paper 19: Rubinstein et al. (2011) — Spinal Manipulative Therapy (SMT)

Citation: Rubinstein SM, van Middelkoop M, Assendelft WJ, de Boer MR, van Tulder MW. Spinal manipulative therapy for chronic low-back pain. Spine. 2011;36(13):E825-46. - Cochrane / Spine Conclusion: SMT produces small, short-term improvements in pain and function that are equivalent to other recommended therapies (exercise therapy, standard medical care, physical therapy), but not clinically superior.

5. Multidisciplinary Rehab & Return to Work (3.5, 3.6, 3.8)

📄 Paper 20: Kamper et al. (2015) — Multidisciplinary Biopsychosocial Rehabilitation

Citation: Kamper SJ, Apeldoorn AT, Chiarotto A, et al. Multidisciplinary biopsychosocial rehabilitation for chronic low back pain: Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ. 2015 Feb 18;350:h444. - Findings: Intensive multidisciplinary biopsychosocial rehabilitation (involving physical, psychological, and social/work interventions by multiple disciplines) is more effective than usual care or physical treatments alone in reducing pain and disability and improving return to work at long-term follow-up (>12 months).

📄 Paper 21: Schaafsma et al. (2013) & Oesch et al. (2010) — Work Conditioning

  • Schaafsma et al. (2013) Cochrane Review: Physical conditioning programs that include cognitive-behavioral components and workplace ergonomics lead to a significant reduction in sick leave days for workers with subacute/chronic LBP compared to usual care.
  • Oesch et al. (2010): High-intensity exercise programs with a work-simulation focus reduce sick leave and work disability.

6. Allied & Complementary Therapies (3.9)

Modality Paper / Evidence Source Primary Cochrane / Systematic Review Finding
Yoga Cramer et al. (2013) Clin J Pain Moderate evidence for short-term and long-term reductions in pain and functional disability in chronic LBP.
Pilates Patti et al. (2015) Medicine Effective in reducing pain and improving functional scores in chronic LBP; similar effect sizes to other active exercise.
MBSR Cramer et al. (2012) BMC CAM Mindfulness-based stress reduction produces significant short-term improvements in pain acceptance and psychological wellbeing.
Osteopathy Franke et al. (2014) BMC MSK Significant reductions in pain and functional improvement in nonspecific LBP compared to sham or control.
Acupuncture Standaert et al. (2011) Spine Provides short-term pain relief in chronic LBP; equivalent or adjunctive to exercise.

7. Section 3 High-Yield MCQ Quick-Check

  1. Nocebo hyperalgesia is mediated by the CCK system and can be blocked by proglumide.
  2. Therapeutic Ultrasound for chronic LBP has no supportive evidence in Cochrane reviews (ineffective).
  3. Graded Exposure explicitly targets fear hierarchies / expectation violations (PHODA), whereas Graded Activity uses operant quota-based progression.
  4. Graded Motor Imagery (GMI) MUST follow the sequential 3 stages: (1) Laterality $\rightarrow$ (2) Motor Imagery $\rightarrow$ (3) Mirror Therapy.
  5. Multidisciplinary Biopsychosocial Rehabilitation is superior to single-modality physical therapy for long-term (>12m) return to work and disability reduction (Kamper 2015).
