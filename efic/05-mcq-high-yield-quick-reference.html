EFIC Diploma in Pain Physiotherapy — High-Yield MCQ Quick-Reference Bible

This quick-reference document distills the essential author names, classification thresholds, Cochrane review verdicts, and classic multiple-choice distractor traps across the entire EFIC syllabus.

1. Landmark Authors & Defining Concepts

2. Quantitative Thresholds & Diagnostic Cut-Offs

  • NRS / VAS MCID (Minimal Clinically Important Difference): $\ge 2$ points on 11-point scale OR $\ge 30\%$ relative pain reduction.
  • DN4 (Douleur Neuropathique 4): Cut-off $\ge 4 / 10$ items indicates neuropathic pain.
  • LANSS (Leeds Assessment of Neuropathic Symptoms): Cut-off $\ge 12 / 24$ points indicates neuropathic pain.
  • CSI (Central Sensitization Inventory): Cut-off $\ge 40 / 100$ indicates central sensitization.
  • Chronic Pain Duration: Pain persisting $>3\text{ months}$ (or past normal healing time).
  • Accelerometry Monitoring Period: Minimum $4\text{–}7\text{ consecutive days}$ (including weekend days) to obtain reliable activity data (Verbunt 2012).
  • Budapest Criteria for CRPS: Requires 1 sign in $\ge 2$ categories AND 1 symptom in $\ge 3$ categories (Sensory, Vasomotor, Sudomotor/Edema, Motor/Trophic).
  • Pediatric Body Maps: Valid starting at age $4\text{–}5\text{ years}$ (Hamill 2014).
  • Pediatric Pain Intensity Scales by Age:
  • <3–4 years: Behavioral observational only (FLACC, NIPS, PIPP).
  • 3–7 years (including <5 years): Faces scales (Wong-Baker FACES, FPS-R).
  • $\ge 8$ years: Self-report numeric/continuous scales (NRS, VAS). Note: Asking "where it hurts" measures location, not intensity!
  • Straight Leg Raise (SLR) Structural Differentiation:
  • Sensitizing maneuvers (increase neural tension): Hip adduction, hip medial (internal) rotation, ankle dorsiflexion/eversion (Bragard / tibial n.), plantarflexion/inversion (peroneal n.), cervical flexion.
  • Desensitizing / slackening maneuver: Flexion of the knee (slacks sciatic nerve immediately; used to differentiate hamstring tightness from neural tension).
  • Dementia Non-Verbal Pain Signs: In non-verbal patients, facial grimacing localized specifically around the eyes and nose (orbit tightening, brow lowering, nose wrinkling - FACS AU 4, 6, 7, 9) is the most specific facial signature of pain.
  • Sleep & Pain Interaction: Bidirectional relationship; improving sleep reduces pain, but only if a critical clinical threshold of sleep improvement is achieved.

3. Top 15 Classic MCQ Distractor Traps

  1. Trap: "Pain is a direct readout of nociception."
  2. Truth: False. Nociception is neither necessary nor sufficient for pain. Pain is a multidimensional perceptual output of the brain.
  3. Trap: "Primary hyperalgesia is driven by spinal dorsal horn sensitization."
  4. Truth: False. Primary hyperalgesia occurs within the injury zone and is peripheral. Secondary hyperalgesia occurs outside the injury zone and is central.
  5. Trap: "Dynamic mechanical allodynia is mediated by $C$-fibers."
  6. Truth: False. Dynamic mechanical allodynia (light brushing) is mediated by low-threshold $A\beta$ mechanoreceptors accessing sensitized spinal circuits.
  7. Trap: "Therapeutic ultrasound is an evidence-based modality for chronic LBP."
  8. Truth: False. Cochrane reviews (Ebadi et al. 2014) show no high-quality evidence of benefit.
  9. Trap: "Graded Motor Imagery can start directly with mirror therapy."
  10. Truth: False. GMI must start with implicit motor imagery (Left/Right laterality recognition), progress to imagined movement, and only then proceed to mirror therapy.
  11. Trap: "Self-report diaries are the gold standard for measuring physical activity in chronic pain."
  12. Truth: False. Self-reports correlate poorly with objective activity (Verbunt 2012); accelerometry is required.
  13. Trap: "In dementia patients, physiological signs (HR/BP) are preferred over behavioral observation."
  14. Truth: False. Physiological signs are non-specific and habituate; validated behavioral observation tools (PAINAD, MOBID-2) are gold standard when self-report is impossible.
  15. Trap: "Blue flags represent psychiatric comorbidities."
  16. Truth: False. Blue flags = workplace perceptions / social factors. Orange flags = psychiatric comorbidities. Yellow flags = psychological beliefs / catastrophizing.
  17. Trap: "Nocebo hyperalgesia only occurs when patients receive explicit deceptive verbal statements."
  18. Truth: False. Implicit cues, conditioning, and clinician diagnostic framing also generate significant nocebo effects via the CCK pathway.
  19. Trap: "Possible neuropathic pain requires confirmatory diagnostic tests."
  20. Truth: False. Possible neuropathic pain requires only a plausible history and neuroanatomical distribution. Confirmatory testing is required for Definite neuropathic pain.
  21. Trap: "Wind-up occurs in peripheral C-fibres and lasts for hours."
  22. Truth: False. Wind-up occurs in dorsal horn wide dynamic range (WDR) neurons (evoked by repetitive C-fibre stimulation at 0.5–2 Hz). It is homosynaptic, lasts only seconds to minutes, and its human psychophysical correlate is Temporal Summation of Pain (TSP). (In contrast, Central Sensitization / LTP is heterosynaptic and lasts hours).
  23. Trap: "All C-fibres are nociceptive and unmyelinated."
  24. Truth: C-fibres are indeed unmyelinated, but NOT all C-fibres are nociceptive (e.g. C-tactile / CT afferents encode pleasant touch; autonomic postganglionics are C-fibres; warm/cool thermoreceptors). Furthermore, peptidergic C-fibres release Substance P and CGRP into peripheral tissues antidromically (neurogenic inflammation / axon reflex). Non-peptidergic C-fibres are purinergic (P2X3 / IB4-binding).
  25. Trap: "Patients with chronic pain cannot improve their health literacy."
  26. Truth: False. Health literacy is modifiable at any stage. Using 'Plain English' and the 'Teach-Back Method' are proven communication strategies. Both provider and patient share responsibility.
  27. Trap: "Bilateral anterior knee pain in an adolescent boy with a normal physical exam is Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)."
  28. Truth: False. JIA requires objective, verifiable inflammatory arthritis (synovitis, joint effusion, heat, limited ROM) lasting >6 weeks. Bilateral anterior knee pain in an adolescent is classically Osgood-Schlatter disease (traction apophysitis) or patellofemoral syndrome; general intra-articular physical examination is normal (except localized tenderness at the tibial tubercle).
  29. Trap: "Nocebo hyperalgesia is mediated by endogenous opioids."

  30. Truth: False. Placebo analgesia is mediated by endogenous opioids and endocannabinoids (blocked by naloxone). Nocebo hyperalgesia is mediated by Cholecystokinin (CCK) and anxiety mechanisms (blocked by CCK-antagonists like proglumide).

  31. Trap: "Gerber's lift-off sign is a test for glenohumeral anterior labral instability."

  32. Truth: False. Gerber's lift-off isolates the subscapularis muscle (part of the rotator cuff). Anterior instability is evaluated with the Apprehension and Relocation test.
  33. Trap: "Bilateral numbness and tingling of both hands and feet is a typical warning sign of Vertebrobasilar Insufficiency (VBI)."
  34. Truth: False. Bilateral tingling of hands/feet indicates cervical spinal cord compression (cervical myelopathy). Classic VBI signs are the 5 D's (Dizziness, Diplopia, Dysarthria, Dysphagia, Drop attacks) and 3 N's (Nausea, Numbness [unilateral facial], Nystagmus).
  35. Trap: "Neuropathic pain above the level of injury is the most common feature of chronic pain after Spinal Cord Injury (SCI)."
  36. Truth: False. Below-level neuropathic pain (central burning pain) is the most common and persistent neuropathic pain in SCI. More than half ($>50\%$) of all SCI patients experience persistent pain, and multiple pain types (nociceptive MSK + neuropathic + visceral) co-exist.
  37. Trap: "The McGill Pain Questionnaire (MPQ) is a validated diagnostic screening tool for detecting neuropathic pain."
  38. Truth: False. The MPQ evaluates general sensory, affective, and evaluative descriptors. Validated neuropathic screening tools are LANSS, DN4, painDETECT, NPQ, and ID-Pain.
  39. Trap: "Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) works by challenging and restructuring distorted cognitive thoughts."
  40. Truth: False. Traditional CBT focuses on cognitive restructuring (changing the content of thoughts). ACT and MBSR focus on psychological flexibility, acceptance, and non-judgmental present-moment awareness without disputing or altering the thoughts themselves.
  41. Trap: "Evidence-based practice dictates that a physiotherapist's personal beliefs about the cause of pain have no measurable effect on their treatment choices."

  42. Truth: False. Systematic reviews (Darlow et al.) prove that physiotherapists' own beliefs strongly guide their clinical management choices (e.g., biomedical beliefs lead to advising rest, off-work certification, and requesting imaging).

  43. Trap: "Manual therapy relieves pain by deactivating peripheral pain receptors."

  44. Truth: False. Manual therapy does NOT mechanically deactivate nociceptors (nor does the concept of a "pain receptor" exist). As formalized in the Bialosky et al. / Sluka model, manual therapy operates through segmental spinal inhibition (Gate Control), descending pain modulatory pathways (PAG-RVM release of opioids, 5-HT, NA), peripheral tissue mechanotransduction (reducing local inflammatory soup), and contextual/psychological factors.
  45. Trap: "According to Vlaeyen and Linton, avoidance behavior and insufficient exposure lead to extinction of the fear response."
  46. Truth: False. In Pavlovian conditioning of pain-related fear (Linton, Flink & Vlaeyen 2018), avoidance and insufficient exposure PREVENT the extinction of threat expectations, thereby locking in the conditioned fear response and chronic disability. (Beware exam questions where sloppy drafting says "insufficient exposure and thus extinction" — the question maker targets the associative learning / extinction concept!).
  47. Trap: "Primary sensory fibers modulate each other directly without intermediate spinal cord circuits."
  48. Truth: False. Axons do not directly synapse on each other; thin (A-delta/C) and thick (A-beta) fibres are modulated by each other via spinal interneurons (Gate Control Theory in the Substantia Gelatinosa).
  49. Trap: "Sleep interventions in chronic pain are always effective, or poor sleep only co-exists with nociplastic pain / depression."
  50. The Exact EFIC Question & Options Breakdown:

    "In explaining the role of sleep in painful conditions to patients, which of the following reflects contemporary knowledge of the interaction between sleep and pain?" - a. Poor sleep and pain only co-exist in people who have nociplastic pain (FALSE: poor sleep occurs across nociceptive, neuropathic, and nociplastic pain; absolutist "only" is wrong) - b. Poor sleep and pain commonly co-exist and sleep interventions may reduce pain if a critical level of sleep improvement is achieved (CORRECT: bidirectional link; sleep therapy relieves pain only once a critical threshold of sleep quality/efficiency improvement is attained) - c. Poor sleep and pain only co-exist in people who have high levels of depression or anxiety (FALSE: sleep-pain interactions occur independently of affective disorders) - d. Poor sleep and pain commonly co-exist but the role sleep plays at an individual level varies and cannot be reliably assessed (FALSE: sleep is reliably assessed via ISI, PSQI, sleep diaries, and actigraphy) - e. Poor sleep and pain commonly co-exist and sleep interventions are always effective at reducing pain (FALSE: absolutist "always"; marginal or failed sleep improvements do not reliably reduce pain)
