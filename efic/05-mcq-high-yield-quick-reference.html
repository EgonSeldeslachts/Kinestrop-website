EFIC Diploma in Pain Physiotherapy — High-Yield MCQ Quick-Reference Bible

This quick-reference document distills the essential author names, classification thresholds, Cochrane review verdicts, and classic multiple-choice distractor traps across the entire EFIC syllabus.

1. Landmark Authors & Defining Concepts

Author & Year Topic Landmark Concept / Takeaway
Finnerup et al. (2016) Neuropathic Pain Updated Grading System: Possible (History+Distribution) $\rightarrow$ Probable (+Sensory Signs) $\rightarrow$ Definite (+Diagnostic Test Confirmation).
Smart et al. (2011) Pain Classification Discriminative validity of 3 mechanisms: Nociceptive, Peripheral Neuropathic, Central Sensitization.
Malfliet et al. (2017) Neuroimaging & Cognition Systematic review (28 studies): Catastrophizing correlates with DLPFC gray matter thinning (impaired top-down gating) & DMN resting-state hyperconnectivity. Reversible via CBT (Seminowicz 2013). Depression/anxiety show no consistent gray matter changes.
Petersen et al. (2014) Nocebo Hyperalgesia Meta-analysis: Moderate-to-large effect size ($g=0.63$), mediated via CCK system (blocked by proglumide).
Moseley & Butler (2015) Pain Education 15 Years of Explaining Pain: Pain is a marker of perceived danger/threat, not tissue damage. Best combined with movement.
Nicholas et al. (2011) "Decade of Flags" Flags framework: Red (pathology), Orange (psychiatric), Yellow (psychological/beliefs), Blue (work perceptions), Black (systemic/insurance).
Veehof et al. (2016) ACT & Mindfulness Meta-analysis: Low-to-high quality evidence for moderate effects specifically on pain interference (SMD 0.62) and anxiety (SMD 0.51). Effects on pain intensity and disability are small.
Macedo et al. (2010) Behavioral Therapy Graded Activity (operant quota-based) vs Graded Exposure (in-vivo fear hierarchy disconfirmation via PHODA).
Geneen et al. (2017) Exercise Overview Cochrane overview: Exercise reduces pain and disability across all chronic pain conditions; safe with minimal adverse events.
Bidonde et al. (2017) Fibromyalgia Cochrane review: Moderate-quality evidence supporting aerobic exercise for HRQoL, function, and pain relief.
Bowering et al. (2013) Graded Motor Imagery 3 sequential stages: (1) Laterality $\rightarrow$ (2) Motor Imagery $\rightarrow$ (3) Mirror Therapy. Efficacy depends on strict sequence.
Ebadi et al. (2014) Ultrasound in LBP Cochrane review: No evidence supporting therapeutic ultrasound for chronic LBP (ineffective).
Rubinstein et al. (2011) SMT in LBP Cochrane review: Spinal manipulation has small effects equivalent to other recommended therapies (exercise), not superior.
Kamper et al. (2015) Multidisciplinary Rehab BMJ / Cochrane: Intensive multidisciplinary biopsychosocial rehab is superior to single-discipline PT for long-term disability and RTW.
Lichtner et al. (2014) Dementia Pain Tools Meta-review of 28 tools: NO SINGLE TOOL is a universal gold standard. Validated behavioral observation during movement (PAINAD, MOBID-2, PACSLAC) is mandatory; physiological signs (HR/BP) habituate and are unreliable.
Wang et al. (2016) Persistent Post-Surgical Pain Meta-analysis ($N=19,813$): Axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) is strongest predictor ($\text{OR}=2.41$); Younger age increases risk ($\text{OR}=1.36$/decade); Radiotherapy ($\text{OR}=1.35$); Acute pain ($\text{OR}=1.16$). Surgery type (mastectomy vs lumpectomy) & chemo have NO effect.
Tyson & Brown (2013) Neurological Pain Scales Tool selection in stroke depends on lesion: Right-hemisphere lesion / neglect $\rightarrow$ Verbal Rating Scales (VRS); Left-hemisphere lesion / aphasia $\rightarrow$ Visual or Numeric Scales (NRS/VAS).
Herr (2011) Geriatric Assessment Assessment hierarchy: Self-report (VRS/NRS) $\rightarrow$ Underlying pathology $\rightarrow$ Behaviors $\rightarrow$ Caregiver proxy $\rightarrow$ Analgesic trial.
Hatfield & Ely (2015) Infant Pain Acute pain tools: FLACC, NIPS, PIPP. Untreated neonatal pain alters future nociceptive neurodevelopment.
Hamill et al. (2014) Pediatric Pain Pain drawings/body maps are valid and reliable in children aged $\ge 4\text{–}5$ years.
Eccleston et al. (2015) Parent Interventions Cochrane review: Interventions targeting parental solicitousness and distress improve child functioning.

2. Quantitative Thresholds & Diagnostic Cut-Offs

  • NRS / VAS MCID (Minimal Clinically Important Difference): $\ge 2$ points on 11-point scale OR $\ge 30\%$ relative pain reduction.
  • DN4 (Douleur Neuropathique 4): Cut-off $\ge 4 / 10$ items indicates neuropathic pain.
  • LANSS (Leeds Assessment of Neuropathic Symptoms): Cut-off $\ge 12 / 24$ points indicates neuropathic pain.
  • CSI (Central Sensitization Inventory): Cut-off $\ge 40 / 100$ indicates central sensitization.
  • Chronic Pain Duration: Pain persisting $>3\text{ months}$ (or past normal healing time).
  • Accelerometry Monitoring Period: Minimum $4\text{–}7\text{ consecutive days}$ (including weekend days) to obtain reliable activity data (Verbunt 2012).
  • Budapest Criteria for CRPS: Requires 1 sign in $\ge 2$ categories AND 1 symptom in $\ge 3$ categories (Sensory, Vasomotor, Sudomotor/Edema, Motor/Trophic).
  • Pediatric Body Maps: Valid starting at age $4\text{–}5\text{ years}$ (Hamill 2014).
  • Pediatric Pain Intensity Scales by Age:
    • <3–4 years: Behavioral observational only (FLACC, NIPS, PIPP).
    • 3–7 years (including <5 years): Faces scales (Wong-Baker FACES, FPS-R).
    • $\ge 8$ years: Self-report numeric/continuous scales (NRS, VAS). Note: Asking "where it hurts" measures location, not intensity!
  • Straight Leg Raise (SLR) Structural Differentiation:
    • Sensitizing maneuvers (increase neural tension): Hip adduction, hip medial (internal) rotation, ankle dorsiflexion/eversion (Bragard / tibial n.), plantarflexion/inversion (peroneal n.), cervical flexion.
    • Desensitizing / slackening maneuver: Flexion of the knee (slacks sciatic nerve immediately; used to differentiate hamstring tightness from neural tension).
  • Dementia Non-Verbal Pain Signs: In non-verbal patients, facial grimacing localized specifically around the eyes and nose (orbit tightening, brow lowering, nose wrinkling - FACS AU 4, 6, 7, 9) is the most specific facial signature of pain.
  • Sleep & Pain Interaction: Bidirectional relationship; improving sleep reduces pain, but only if a critical clinical threshold of sleep improvement is achieved.

3. Top 15 Classic MCQ Distractor Traps

  1. Trap: "Pain is a direct readout of nociception."
    • Truth: False. Nociception is neither necessary nor sufficient for pain. Pain is a multidimensional perceptual output of the brain.
  2. Trap: "Primary hyperalgesia is driven by spinal dorsal horn sensitization."
    • Truth: False. Primary hyperalgesia occurs within the injury zone and is peripheral. Secondary hyperalgesia occurs outside the injury zone and is central.
  3. Trap: "Dynamic mechanical allodynia is mediated by $C$-fibers."
    • Truth: False. Dynamic mechanical allodynia (light brushing) is mediated by low-threshold $A\beta$ mechanoreceptors accessing sensitized spinal circuits.
  4. Trap: "Therapeutic ultrasound is an evidence-based modality for chronic LBP."
    • Truth: False. Cochrane reviews (Ebadi et al. 2014) show no high-quality evidence of benefit.
  5. Trap: "Graded Motor Imagery can start directly with mirror therapy."
    • Truth: False. GMI must start with implicit motor imagery (Left/Right laterality recognition), progress to imagined movement, and only then proceed to mirror therapy.
  6. Trap: "Self-report diaries are the gold standard for measuring physical activity in chronic pain."
    • Truth: False. Self-reports correlate poorly with objective activity (Verbunt 2012); accelerometry is required.
  7. Trap: "In dementia patients, physiological signs (HR/BP) are preferred over behavioral observation."
    • Truth: False. Physiological signs are non-specific and habituate; validated behavioral observation tools (PAINAD, MOBID-2) are gold standard when self-report is impossible.
  8. Trap: "Blue flags represent psychiatric comorbidities."
    • Truth: False. Blue flags = workplace perceptions / social factors. Orange flags = psychiatric comorbidities. Yellow flags = psychological beliefs / catastrophizing.
  9. Trap: "Nocebo hyperalgesia only occurs when patients receive explicit deceptive verbal statements."
    • Truth: False. Implicit cues, conditioning, and clinician diagnostic framing also generate significant nocebo effects via the CCK pathway.
  10. Trap: "Possible neuropathic pain requires confirmatory diagnostic tests."
    • Truth: False. Possible neuropathic pain requires only a plausible history and neuroanatomical distribution. Confirmatory testing is required for Definite neuropathic pain.
  11. Trap: "Wind-up occurs in peripheral C-fibres and lasts for hours."
    • Truth: False. Wind-up occurs in dorsal horn wide dynamic range (WDR) neurons (evoked by repetitive C-fibre stimulation at 0.5–2 Hz). It is homosynaptic, lasts only seconds to minutes, and its human psychophysical correlate is Temporal Summation of Pain (TSP). (In contrast, Central Sensitization / LTP is heterosynaptic and lasts hours).
  12. Trap: "All C-fibres are nociceptive and unmyelinated."
    • Truth: C-fibres are indeed unmyelinated, but NOT all C-fibres are nociceptive (e.g. C-tactile / CT afferents encode pleasant touch; autonomic postganglionics are C-fibres; warm/cool thermoreceptors). Furthermore, peptidergic C-fibres release Substance P and CGRP into peripheral tissues antidromically (neurogenic inflammation / axon reflex). Non-peptidergic C-fibres are purinergic (P2X3 / IB4-binding).
  13. Trap: "Patients with chronic pain cannot improve their health literacy."
    • Truth: False. Health literacy is modifiable at any stage. Using 'Plain English' and the 'Teach-Back Method' are proven communication strategies. Both provider and patient share responsibility.
  14. Trap: "Bilateral anterior knee pain in an adolescent boy with a normal physical exam is Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)."
    • Truth: False. JIA requires objective, verifiable inflammatory arthritis (synovitis, joint effusion, heat, limited ROM) lasting >6 weeks. Bilateral anterior knee pain in an adolescent is classically Osgood-Schlatter disease (traction apophysitis) or patellofemoral syndrome; general intra-articular physical examination is normal (except localized tenderness at the tibial tubercle).

  15. Trap: "Nocebo hyperalgesia is mediated by endogenous opioids."

    • Truth: False. Placebo analgesia is mediated by endogenous opioids and endocannabinoids (blocked by naloxone). Nocebo hyperalgesia is mediated by Cholecystokinin (CCK) and anxiety mechanisms (blocked by CCK-antagonists like proglumide).

  16. Trap: "Gerber's lift-off sign is a test for glenohumeral anterior labral instability."

    • Truth: False. Gerber's lift-off isolates the subscapularis muscle (part of the rotator cuff). Anterior instability is evaluated with the Apprehension and Relocation test.
  17. Trap: "Bilateral numbness and tingling of both hands and feet is a typical warning sign of Vertebrobasilar Insufficiency (VBI)."
    • Truth: False. Bilateral tingling of hands/feet indicates cervical spinal cord compression (cervical myelopathy). Classic VBI signs are the 5 D's (Dizziness, Diplopia, Dysarthria, Dysphagia, Drop attacks) and 3 N's (Nausea, Numbness [unilateral facial], Nystagmus).
  18. Trap: "Neuropathic pain above the level of injury is the most common feature of chronic pain after Spinal Cord Injury (SCI)."
    • Truth: False. Below-level neuropathic pain (central burning pain) is the most common and persistent neuropathic pain in SCI. More than half ($>50\%$) of all SCI patients experience persistent pain, and multiple pain types (nociceptive MSK + neuropathic + visceral) co-exist.
  19. Trap: "The McGill Pain Questionnaire (MPQ) is a validated diagnostic screening tool for detecting neuropathic pain."
    • Truth: False. The MPQ evaluates general sensory, affective, and evaluative descriptors. Validated neuropathic screening tools are LANSS, DN4, painDETECT, NPQ, and ID-Pain.
  20. Trap: "Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) works by challenging and restructuring distorted cognitive thoughts."
    • Truth: False. Traditional CBT focuses on cognitive restructuring (changing the content of thoughts). ACT and MBSR focus on psychological flexibility, acceptance, and non-judgmental present-moment awareness without disputing or altering the thoughts themselves.
  21. Trap: "Evidence-based practice dictates that a physiotherapist's personal beliefs about the cause of pain have no measurable effect on their treatment choices."
    • Truth: False. Systematic reviews (Darlow et al.) prove that physiotherapists' own beliefs strongly guide their clinical management choices (e.g., biomedical beliefs lead to advising rest, off-work certification, and requesting imaging).
