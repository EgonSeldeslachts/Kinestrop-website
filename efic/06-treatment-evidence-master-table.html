Section 3 & 4: Treatment Evidence & Effect Size Master Table — EFIC Study Bible

This document is the granular clinical evidence and effect-size synthesis for the EFIC European Diploma in Pain Physiotherapy examination.

To answer EFIC multiple-choice questions correctly, you must memorize: 1. The exact GRADE / Cochrane evidence quality rating (e.g., Moderate-quality, Low-to-high, Inconclusive, No evidence). 2. The exact outcome-specific effect sizes (Differentiating Pain Intensity from Pain Interference, Disability, Depression, Anxiety, and Work / RTW). 3. The durability of effect (Short-term post-treatment vs. Long-term $\ge 12\text{ months}$). 4. The "⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula" provided for each study.

1. Education, Communication & Expectation

1.1 Detailed Evidence Matrix

📄 Petersen et al. (2014) — Meta-Analysis of Nocebo Hyperalgesia

  • Citation: Pain, 155(8):1426–34.
  • Design & Sample: Meta-analysis of 10 experimental laboratory studies ($N = 524$).
  • Evidence Quality: High internal validity (experimental randomized laboratory models).
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Overall Nocebo Hyperalgesia: Moderate-to-large effect (Hedges' $g = 0.63$, 95% CI 0.49–0.78). - Conditioning + Negative Verbal Suggestion: Moderate-to-large effect ($g = 0.77$). - Negative Verbal Suggestion Alone: Moderate effect ($g = 0.52$). - Conditioning Alone: Small-to-moderate effect ($g = 0.43$).
  • Neurobiological Mechanism: Mediated by Cholecystokinin (CCK) and HPA axis activation; completely blocked or reversed by CCK antagonists (proglumide).
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    High internal validity | Moderate-to-large effect (g = 0.63) on pain sensitivity | Verbal + conditioning (g = 0.77) > Verbal alone (g = 0.52) | CCK-mediated, blocked by proglumide.

📄 Moseley & Butler (2015) — 15 Years of Explaining Pain

  • Citation: J Pain, 16(9):807–13.
  • Design & Sample: Narrative synthesis and meta-analytic overview of Pain Neuroscience Education (PNE).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate-to-high quality across multiple clinical trials.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Biology Knowledge: Large effect ($\text{SMD} > 0.80$). - Pain Catastrophizing (PCS) & Kinesiophobia (TSK): Moderate-to-large reduction ($\text{SMD} \approx -0.50\text{ to } -0.70$). - Pain Intensity (Standalone PNE): Small reduction ($\text{SMD} \approx -0.25$). - Pain Intensity (PNE + Active Movement): Moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} \approx -0.50$). - Disability / Function (Standalone PNE): Small / Non-significant effect. - Disability / Function (PNE + Active Movement): Moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} \approx -0.45$). - Healthcare Utilization: Moderate reduction in doctor consultations and diagnostic imaging requests.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate-to-high quality | Large effect on knowledge | Moderate-to-large reduction in catastrophizing & kinesiophobia | Small effect on pain/disability as standalone → Moderate effect when combined with movement.

📄 Geneen et al. (2015) — Systematic Review of Pain Education in Chronic Pain

  • Citation: Syst Rev, 4:132.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs ($N = 2,999$ adults with chronic pain).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Biology Knowledge: Large improvement ($\text{SMD} = 0.81$, 95% CI 0.53–1.08). - Pain Intensity (Short-term $\le 3\text{m}$): Small reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.29$, 95% CI -0.46 to -0.12). - Physical Function / Disability: Small / Non-significant standalone effect ($\text{SMD} = -0.14$). - Self-Efficacy: Small-to-moderate improvement ($\text{SMD} = 0.38$).
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Large effect on knowledge (SMD 0.81) | Small effect on short-term pain (SMD -0.29) | No significant standalone effect on disability.

📄 Engers et al. (2008) — Cochrane Review of Patient Education for Low Back Pain

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (1):CD003005.
  • Design & Sample: 24 RCTs ($N = 4,923$).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate-to-high quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Acute Low Back Pain: - Brief patient education (e.g., The Back Book, 2-page advice sheet to stay active): Moderate effect on reducing sick leave and healthcare consultations; Small effect on pain. - Chronic Low Back Pain: - Brief generic advice/booklets: NO effect / Ineffective for pain or disability. - Intensive 1-on-1 or group biopsychosocial education: Moderate effect on functional disability and return to work.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate-to-high quality | Acute LBP: Brief advice/booklet effective for sick leave | Chronic LBP: Brief advice INEFFECTIVE; intensive 1-on-1 biopsychosocial education required.

2. Behavioural, Psychological & Cognitive Interventions

2.1 Detailed Evidence Matrix

📄 Veehof et al. (2016) — Meta-Analysis of Acceptance & Mindfulness-Based Interventions

  • Citation: Cogn Behav Ther, 45(1):5–31.
  • Design & Sample: Meta-analysis of 25 RCTs ($N = 1,285$ chronic pain patients) evaluating ACT, MBSR, and MBCT.
  • Evidence Quality: Low-to-high quality (heterogeneous; 6 scored high, 5 scored low, none met all criteria; mean quality did not moderate effect).
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Interference: - Post-treatment: Moderate effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.62$, 95% CI 0.43–0.81). - Follow-up (2–6 months): Large effect ($\text{SMD} > 0.80$). - Anxiety: Moderate effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.51$, 95% CI 0.32–0.70). - Pain Intensity: Small effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.37$, 95% CI 0.24–0.49). - Depression: Small effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.35$, 95% CI 0.20–0.51). - Disability: Small effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.31$, 95% CI 0.15–0.47). - Quality of Life: Small effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.39$, non-significant).
  • Subgroup Findings: ACT demonstrated significantly higher effect sizes on depression and anxiety than MBSR or MBCT.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Low-to-high quality evidence | Moderate effects on PAIN INTERFERENCE (SMD 0.62) and ANXIETY (SMD 0.51) | Small effects on pain intensity, depression, and disability | Pain interference effect grows to Large at follow-up.

📄 Macedo et al. (2010) — Graded Activity vs. Graded Exposure in Chronic LBP

  • Citation: Phys Ther, 90(6):860–79.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review of 13 RCTs ($N = 1,540$).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Intensity & Disability: Both Graded Activity (GA) and Graded Exposure (GExp) produce small-to-moderate improvements over minimal care. - Catastrophizing & Fear of Movement (TSK): Moderate-to-large reduction favoring Graded Exposure over Graded Activity in patients with high baseline fear. - Long-term Follow-up (12 months): Pain and disability outcomes between GA and GExp are equivalent.
  • Mechanisms: - Graded Activity: Operant conditioning using quota-based, time-contingent progression regardless of pain. - Graded Exposure: Cognitive-behavioral in-vivo confrontation based on fear hierarchies (e.g., PHODA) to violate threat expectations.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | GA and GExp equivalent for pain & disability at 12m | GExp superior for reducing catastrophizing & fear-avoidance in high-fear patients | GA = Operant quota; GExp = In-vivo fear hierarchy (PHODA).

📄 Henschke et al. (2010) — Cochrane Review of Behavioural Treatment for Chronic LBP

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (7):CD002014.
  • Design & Sample: 30 RCTs ($N = 3,438$).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Relief (vs. Waitlist): Moderate short-term effect ($\text{SMD} = -0.62$, 95% CI -0.88 to -0.35). - Functional Improvement / Disability (vs. Waitlist): Moderate short-term effect ($\text{SMD} = -0.45$). - Pain & Function (vs. Active PT / Usual Care): Small / Negligible difference when delivered in isolation. - Behavioral Therapy + Active Physical Exercise: Moderate long-term reduction in pain and disability. - Operant vs. Cognitive vs. Respondent: No significant difference between behavioral sub-types.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Moderate short-term effect vs waitlist (pain SMD -0.62, disability SMD -0.45) | Behavioral + Exercise superior to PT alone long-term | No difference between operant, cognitive, or respondent types.

📄 Eccleston et al. (2014) — Cochrane Review of Internet-Delivered Psychological Therapies

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (2):CD010152.
  • Design & Sample: 15 RCTs ($N = 2,012$ adults with chronic non-cancer pain).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Intensity (Post-treatment): Small reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.37$, 95% CI -0.59 to -0.15). - Disability / Function (Post-treatment): Moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.50$, 95% CI -0.79 to -0.22). - Depression: Small-to-moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.26$). - Anxiety: Small-to-moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.33$). - Delivery Format: Therapist-supported internet CBT yielded significantly higher completion rates ($>75\%$) and larger effect sizes than unguided automated self-help.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Moderate effect on disability (SMD -0.50) | Small effect on pain (SMD -0.37) and mood | Therapist-supported >> Unguided automated modules.

📄 Persson et al. (2008) — Relaxation for Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain

  • Citation: Phys Ther Rev, 13(5):355–365.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review of 15 RCTs ($N = 963$).
  • Evidence Quality: Low-to-moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Intensity: Small short-term reduction ($\text{SMD} \approx -0.30$). - Muscle Guarding / Autonomic Arousal: Moderate short-term reduction. - Long-term Functional Recovery / Sick Leave: No significant effect / Inconclusive.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Low-to-moderate quality | Small short-term pain relief | Moderate reduction in somatic/muscle tension | Ineffective as standalone for long-term disability or work resumption.

📄 O'Keeffe et al. (2016) — Physical vs. Behavioral vs. Combined in Spinal Pain

  • Citation: J Pain, 17(7):755–74.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review & meta-analysis of 56 RCTs ($N = 8,713$).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Combined Interventions (Physical + Behavioral / Psychologically Informed): - Disability: Moderate effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.48$). - Pain: Moderate effect ($\text{SMD} = 0.43$). - Isolated Physical Interventions: Small-to-moderate short-term effects; lower 12-month durability. - Isolated Behavioral Interventions: Small-to-moderate on disability, minimal effect on physical capacity.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Combined (Physical + Psychologically informed) superior to either alone | Disability SMD 0.48, Pain SMD 0.43 | Highest long-term durability.

📄 Nicholas et al. (2011) & Linton & Shaw (2011) — The Flags Hierarchy

  • Citation: Phys Ther, 91(5):737–53 & 91(5):700–11.
  • Evidence Quality: Landmark epidemiological consensus framework.
  • The Flags Hierarchy for MCQ: - Red Flags: Serious biomedical pathology (infection, fracture, malignancy, cauda equina). - Orange Flags: Psychiatric illness (clinical depression, psychosis, personality disorders). - Yellow Flags: Psychological & cognitive beliefs (fear-avoidance, catastrophizing, low self-efficacy, expectation of harm). - Blue Flags: Workplace perceptions & social factors (unsupportive boss, high job demand, low control). - Black Flags: Systemic, financial, and legal barriers (workers' comp litigation, insurance delays, rigid sickness certification).
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Red = Pathology | Orange = Psychiatric | Yellow = Cognitive beliefs & fear | Blue = Workplace perceptions | Black = Systemic/Legal/Insurance.

3. Exercise & Physical Activity Prescription

3.1 Detailed Evidence Matrix

📄 Geneen et al. (2017) — Cochrane Overview of Exercise in Chronic Pain

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 4:CD011279.
  • Design & Sample: Overview of 21 Cochrane reviews, 264 RCTs ($N = 19,642$).
  • Evidence Quality: Low-to-moderate quality across diverse reviews.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Severity: Small-to-moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} \approx -0.35\text{ to } -0.50$). - Physical Function: Small-to-moderate improvement ($\text{SMD} \approx 0.30\text{ to } 0.50$). - Quality of Life (HRQoL): Small improvement ($\text{SMD} \approx 0.25$). - Adverse Events: Predominantly minor and transient (muscle soreness). Exercise is safe and first-line. - Modality Superiority: No single exercise type (aerobic vs. resistance vs. motor control) is consistently superior across all chronic pain syndromes.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Low-to-moderate quality overview | Small-to-moderate reduction in pain and disability across all pain conditions | Highly safe | No single exercise type universally superior.

📄 Bidonde et al. (2017) — Cochrane Review of Aerobic Exercise in Fibromyalgia

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 6:CD009747.
  • Design & Sample: 13 RCTs ($N = 1,264$ adults with fibromyalgia).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate-quality evidence.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQoL on 0–100 FIQ): Moderate clinically meaningful improvement (Mean Difference $\text{MD} = -5.6\text{ points}$, 95% CI -8.6 to -2.6). - Pain Intensity (0–100 VAS): Small-to-moderate reduction ($\text{MD} = -8.8\text{ points}$, 95% CI -13.7 to -3.9). - Physical Function (0–100 scale): Moderate improvement ($\text{MD} = -6.4\text{ points}$). - Fatigue & Stiffness: Small / Borderline improvement. - Prescription Rule: Low-to-moderate intensity (walking, cycling, aquatic), progressing slowly to avoid post-exertional flare-ups.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    MODERATE-QUALITY evidence | Moderate improvement in HRQoL (MD -5.6 on FIQ) and function (MD -6.4) | Small-to-moderate pain reduction (MD -8.8) | Low-to-moderate intensity with gradual progression.

📄 Searle et al. (2015) — Exercise Modalities in Chronic Low Back Pain

  • Citation: Clin Rehabil, 29(12):1155–67.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review & meta-analysis of 39 RCTs ($N = 4,144$).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Intensity: - Motor Control Exercise: Moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.45$, 95% CI -0.61 to -0.29). - Resistance / Strengthening: Moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.42$). - Aerobic Exercise: Small reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.28$). - Stretching / Flexibility: Small reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.25$). - Disability / Function: - Motor Control Exercise: Moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.41$). - Resistance / Strengthening: Small-to-moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.38$). - Long-term Equivalence: Differences diminish at 12 months; general physical activity yields comparable long-term functional recovery.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Motor control (SMD -0.45) & Resistance (SMD -0.42) have slightly higher short-term effect sizes than aerobic (SMD -0.28) | Equivalence achieved at 12 months.

📄 Oliveira et al. (2016) — Physical Activity vs. Exercise Therapy in MSK Pain

  • Citation: Arthritis Care Res, 68(12):1832–1842.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review & meta-analysis of 15 RCTs ($N = 1,388$).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Objectively Measured Physical Activity (Accelerometry): - Interventions specifically targeting daily physical activity: Moderate increase (Mean Difference $\text{MD} = +1,210\text{ steps/day}$, 95% CI 479 to 1,941). - Routine supervised clinical exercise therapy: NO significant change in daily step count or habitual activity levels.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Clinical exercise does NOT automatically increase daily physical activity | Interventions targeting daily habits increase steps by +1,210 steps/day | Accelerometry (4-7 days) required for objective tracking.

📄 Booth et al. (2017) — Biopsychosocial Exercise Prescription

  • Citation: Musculoskelet Care, 15(4):413–421.
  • Evidence Quality: Clinical framework & mechanistic synthesis.
  • Core Findings for MCQ: - Exercise-Induced Hypoalgesia (EIH): Healthy individuals experience reduced pain sensitivity post-exercise via endogenous opioid/endocannabinoid release. Patients with central sensitization often display dysfunctional EIH (hyperalgesia/flare-up post-exercise). - Clinical Strategy: De-emphasize biomechanical alignment and tissue damage; emphasize safe movement, graded exposure, and nervous system desensitization with very low initial thresholds.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Central sensitization causes dysfunctional EIH (post-exercise hyperalgesia) | Exercise acts via central neuro-desensitization, NOT tissue biomechanical fixing.

📄 Paper 15c: Wieland et al. (2017) — Yoga for Chronic Low Back Pain

Citation: Wieland LS, Skoetz N, Pilkington K, Vempati R, D'Adamo CR, Berman BM. Yoga treatment for chronic non-specific low back pain. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2017 Jan 12;1(1):CD010671. - Evidence Quality: Low-to-moderate quality across 12 RCTs ($N=1,080$). - Quantified Findings: - Back-specific function: Moderate improvement at 3 months ($ ext{SMD} = -0.44$, 95% CI -0.66 to -0.22). - Pain intensity: Small-to-moderate reduction ($ ext{MD} = -4.55/100 ext{ mm}$ at 3m; $-7.81/100$ at 6m). - Clinically equivalent to other active physical therapy exercise programs; superior to non-exercise educational control. - ⚡ MCQ Takeaway: Low-to-moderate evidence for small-to-moderate pain and functional improvements; equivalent to standard PT exercise.

4. Passive Modalities, Manual Therapy & Neuromodulation

4.1 Detailed Evidence Matrix

📄 Ebadi et al. (2014) — Cochrane Review of Therapeutic Ultrasound for Chronic LBP

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (3):CD009169.
  • Design & Sample: 7 RCTs ($N = 362$).
  • Evidence Quality: Low-to-moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Intensity vs. Placebo/Sham: No clinically meaningful difference ($\text{MD} = 2.16\text{ mm}$ on 100mm VAS, 95% CI -4.66 to 8.98). - Disability vs. Placebo/Sham: No statistically significant difference ($\text{MD} = 0.41$ on 0–24 Roland-Morris scale). - Clinical Verdict: NO supportive evidence; Therapeutic ultrasound is INEFFECTIVE and NOT recommended.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    🚨 CLASSIC NEGATIVE TRAP: Low-to-moderate quality | NO evidence of benefit over sham (MD 2.16mm) | INEFFECTIVE and NOT recommended for chronic LBP.

📄 Rubinstein et al. (2011) — Cochrane Review of Spinal Manipulation (SMT) for Chronic LBP

  • Citation: Spine / Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 36(13):E825–46.
  • Design & Sample: 26 RCTs ($N = 6,070$).
  • Evidence Quality: High-to-moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Short-term Pain Relief (1 month): Small reduction ($\text{MD} = -4.5\text{ mm}$ on 100mm VAS, 95% CI -7.3 to -1.7). - Short-term Disability (1 month): Small reduction ($\text{MD} = -2.1\text{ points}$ on 0–100 scale). - Equivalence: SMT is equivalent to active exercise therapy, standard physiotherapy, and usual medical care. It is NOT superior. - Long-term Follow-up (12 months): No statistically significant difference in pain or disability compared to other active therapies.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    High-to-moderate quality | Small short-term effect on pain (MD -4.5mm) and disability (MD -2.1) | Equivalent to exercise and PT, NOT superior | No long-term advantage.

📄 Johnson et al. (2015) — Cochrane Review of TENS for Phantom Limb Pain

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (8):CD007264.
  • Design & Sample: 0 high-quality RCTs meeting inclusion criteria; narrative review.
  • Evidence Quality: Very low quality / INCONCLUSIVE.
  • Clinical Verdict: Formal evidence from randomized trials is inconclusive. TENS is justified as a pragmatic, low-risk empirical trial in individual patients, but definitive Cochrane confirmation of efficacy is lacking.
  • Neurobiology (from Sluka 2016): - High-frequency TENS ($>50\text{ Hz}$): Activates large $A\beta$ fibers $\rightarrow$ stimulates $\delta$-opioid receptors in spinal dorsal horn and RVM. - Low-frequency TENS ($<10\text{ Hz}$ / acupuncture-like): Stimulates motor and $A\delta$ fibers $\rightarrow$ stimulates $\mu$-opioid receptors in spinal cord and brainstem.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Very low / INCONCLUSIVE Cochrane evidence in phantom limb pain | Neurobiology: High-freq TENS = δ-opioid receptors; Low-freq TENS = μ-opioid receptors.

📄 Bowering et al. (2013) — Systematic Review of Graded Motor Imagery (GMI)

  • Citation: J Pain, 14(1):3–13.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review & meta-analysis of 6 RCTs ($N = 180$) in CRPS I and Phantom Limb Pain.
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Intensity & Disability: Large effect size (Hedges' $g = 0.81$, 95% CI 0.42–1.20) favoring sequential GMI over standard medical care or usual physiotherapy. - Sequential Order Rule: Benefits occur ONLY when the 3 stages are delivered sequentially: 1. Implicit Motor Imagery: Left/Right Laterality Recognition. 2. Explicit Motor Imagery: Imagined movements. 3. Mirror Therapy: Visual feedback of unaffected limb. - Sequence Disruption: Introducing mirror therapy without prior implicit motor training can provoke symptom flares and increase cortical disorganization.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Large effect size (g = 0.81) for pain & disability in CRPS I and phantom pain | Efficacy strictly depends on sequence: (1) Laterality → (2) Motor Imagery → (3) Mirror Therapy.

📄 Paper 19b: Jensen, Day, & Miró (2014) — Neuromodulatory Treatments for Chronic Pain

Citation: Jensen MP, Day MA, Miró J. Neuromodulatory treatments for chronic pain: efficacy and mechanisms. Nat Rev Neurol. 2014 Mar;10(3):167-78. - Evidence Level: Moderate-quality mechanistic and clinical review (tDCS, rTMS, neurofeedback). - Quantified Takeaways: Non-invasive brain stimulation over primary motor cortex ($M1$) alters thalamocortical dysrhythmia, delivering modest transient pain reduction ($ ext{SMD} pprox -0.38 ext{ to }-0.45$). - Synergy: Efficacy and durability are significantly augmented when paired with active motor retraining. - ⚡ MCQ Takeaway: $M1$ stimulation modulates thalamocortical networks; optimal when combined with active exercise.

5. Multidisciplinary & Occupational Rehabilitation

5.1 Detailed Evidence Matrix

📄 Kamper et al. (2015) — Cochrane / BMJ Review of Multidisciplinary Biopsychosocial Rehab

  • Citation: BMJ, 350:h444 / Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (9):CD000963.
  • Design & Sample: 41 RCTs ($N = 6,858$ adults with chronic low back pain).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate-to-high quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Pain Reduction (at $>12\text{ months}$ vs. Usual Care): Moderate effect ($\text{SMD} = -0.51$, 95% CI -0.67 to -0.34). - Pain Reduction (at $>12\text{ months}$ vs. Physical Treatments Alone): Small-to-moderate effect ($\text{SMD} = -0.21$). - Disability Reduction (at $>12\text{ months}$ vs. Physical Treatments Alone): Small-to-moderate effect ($\text{SMD} = -0.23$). - Work Status / Return to Work (at $>12\text{ months}$ vs. Physical Treatments Alone): Significantly higher odds of working (Odds Ratio $\text{OR} = 1.87$, 95% CI 1.39 to 2.53).
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate-to-high quality | Multidisciplinary rehab is superior to monodisciplinary PT specifically at LONG-TERM (>12 months) follow-up | Significantly increases return to work (OR 1.87).

📄 Schaafsma et al. (2013) — Cochrane Review of Physical Conditioning for Work Outcomes

  • Citation: Cochrane Database Syst Rev, (8):CD001822.
  • Design & Sample: 23 RCTs ($N = 3,676$ workers with back pain).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Sick Leave Days at 12 Months: Significant reduction (Mean Difference $\text{MD} = -45\text{ sick leave days}$, 95% CI -72 to -18) in subacute/chronic workers compared to usual care. - Essential Component: Physical conditioning is effective only when workplace visits, ergonomics, and cognitive-behavioral work simulation are incorporated.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Reduces sick leave by average 45 days at 12 months in subacute/chronic LBP | Must include cognitive-behavioral work simulation and workplace integration.

📄 Oesch et al. (2010) — Exercise & Work Disability in Persistent LBP

  • Citation: J Rehabil Med, 42(3):193–205.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review & meta-analysis of 23 RCTs ($N = 3,614$).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Outcome-by-Outcome Quantified Effect Sizes: - Work Disability / Days Off Work: Small-to-moderate reduction ($\text{SMD} = -0.38$). High-intensity functional work conditioning outperformed low-intensity general exercise for work retention.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Work disability reduced (SMD -0.38) | High-intensity work-simulated exercise > Low-intensity general exercise for RTW.

📄 Richards et al. (2013) — Physiotherapy Functional Restoration in Post-Acute LBP

  • Citation: Man Ther, 18(1):4–25.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review of 13 RCTs ($N = 2,154$).

📄 Karp et al. (2008) & Herr (2011) — Pain Assessment & Pharmacology in Older Adults

  • Citation: Br J Anaesth, 101(1):111–20 & J Pain, 12(3 Suppl 1):S3–S13.
  • Evidence Quality: Geriatric pain management consensus.
  • Core Clinical Rules for MCQ: - Pharmacokinetics / Pharmacodynamics: Decreased renal clearance (GFR) and hepatic CYP450 metabolism $\rightarrow$ heightened drug accumulation and toxicity. - NSAIDs: Severe risks of GI ulceration/bleeding, acute renal failure, worsening hypertension, and congestive heart failure. - Opioids & TCAs: Severe risks of sedation, cognitive impairment, delirium, and falls. 📄 Lichtner et al. (2014) — Pain Assessment for People with Dementia: Meta-Review of Tools

  • Citation: BMC Geriatr, 14:138. DOI: 10.1186/1471-2318-14-138.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review of systematic reviews (meta-review) evaluating 28 observational pain assessment tools across 23 reviews (8 providing extractable psychometric data).
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality meta-review.
  • Key Evaluated Tools: - PAINAD (Pain Assessment in Advanced Dementia): 5 items (Breathing, Negative vocalization, Facial expression, Body language, Consolability); rapid (2–5 min), high inter-rater reliability, widely used in clinical nursing. - MOBID-2 (Mobility-Observed Information Dementia Pain Scale): Evaluates pain during active and guided movements across different body parts; highly sensitive to musculoskeletal pain. - PACSLAC (Pain Assessment Checklist for Seniors with Limited Ability to Communicate): Comprehensive checklist (60 items across 4 subscales); high validity, but more time-consuming. - Abbey Pain Scale & DOLOPLUS-2: Widely cited European observational scales; variable reporting of psychometric rigor.
  • Core Findings for MCQ: - No Single Gold Standard: Due to variability in study quality and lack of an objective reference standard in non-verbal patients, no single tool can be definitively recommended as superior over all others. - Behavioral vs. Physiological: Autonomic/physiological signs (HR, BP) are unreliable and non-specific; validated observational behavioral tools are mandatory. - Movement-Based Observation: Pain behaviors are significantly more apparent during guided movement/nursing care (as in MOBID-2) than at rest.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Meta-review of 28 tools | NO SINGLE TOOL can be recommended as the universal gold standard | Behavioral observation during movement (MOBID-2/PAINAD) >> Physiological signs.

📄 Hamill et al. (2014) — Pain-Location Tools (Body Maps) for Children

  • Citation: Pain, 155(8):1448–58. DOI: 10.1016/j.pain.2014.04.016.

  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.

  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Moderate quality | Pediatric body maps are valid & reliable from age 4–5 years and up | Under age 4 cannot reliably map pain | Electronic maps equal or superior to paper.

📄 Hatfield & Ely (2015) — Measurement of Acute Pain in Infants

8.1 Detailed Assessment & Prognosis Matrix

📄 Wang et al. (2016) — Predictors of Persistent Post-Surgical Pain (PPSP) After Breast Cancer Surgery

📄 Tyson & Brown (2013) — Measuring Pain in Neurological Conditions (Stroke, MS, SCI)

  • Citation: Clin Rehabil, 28(7):669–86. DOI: 10.1177/0269215513512217.
  • Design & Sample: Systematic review of 13 studies evaluating 11 pain measurement tools in central nervous system disorders.
  • Evidence Quality: Moderate quality.
  • Core Findings for MCQ: - Psychometric Deficits: Few pain tools have been adequately tested for reliability and responsiveness specifically in central neurological conditions. - Hemispheric Stroke Tool Selection Rule: - Right-hemisphere lesions (visuospatial deficits, unilateral neglect, body schema distortion): Patients perform poorly on visual scales; prefer verbal rating scales (VRS) or written descriptors. - Left-hemisphere lesions (aphasia, verbal communication impairment): Patients cannot use verbal/written scales; prefer numeric (NRS), visual (VAS), or pictorial/faces scales.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Neurological pain tools lack robust psychometric testing | Right brain lesion / neglect = Use VERBAL scales (avoid visual) | Left brain lesion / aphasia = Use VISUAL/NUMERIC scales (avoid verbal).

📄 Gordon (2015) — Acute Pain Assessment: Moving Beyond Simple Pain Ratings

  • Citation: Curr Opin Anaesthesiol, 28(5):565–9. DOI: 10.1097/ACO.0000000000000225.
  • Evidence Quality: Clinical practice and health policy review.
  • Core Findings for MCQ: - Treating pain strictly as the "fifth vital sign" using solitary 0–10 Numeric Rating Scales led to over-reliance on opioid prescribing and failed to evaluate physical recovery. - Acute pain assessment must evaluate functional interference (e.g., ability to deep breathe, cough, ambulate, and sleep) rather than pursuing a zero pain score at rest.

📄 Verbunt et al. (2012) — Physical Activity Assessment by Movement Registration Systems

  • Citation: Clin J Pain, 28(7):643–54. DOI: 10.1097/AJP.0b013e31823bb35c.
  • Design & Sample: Methodological and psychometric review of accelerometry and activity monitors in chronic pain.
  • Core Findings for MCQ: - Subjective vs. Objective Discordance: Self-report diaries and questionnaires correlate poorly with objective accelerometry; patients with high pain catastrophizing and fear-avoidance systematically overestimate or underestimate their true physical activity. - Monitoring Duration: A minimum of 4 to 7 consecutive days (including weekdays and weekends) of triaxial accelerometry is required to achieve reliable, representative physical activity profiling.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Self-reports correlate poorly with objective activity | Valid accelerometry requires 4 to 7 consecutive days of continuous measurement.

📄 Smart et al. (2011) — Clinical Discriminative Validity: Mechanisms-Based Pain Classification

  • Citation: Man Ther, 16(4):302–8 & 17(4):336–44.
  • Design & Sample: Delphi consensus and clinical validation studies ($N = 464$) establishing clinical criteria for mechanism-based pain classification.
  • Evidence Quality: High-to-moderate clinical validity.
  • The Three Mechanism Clusters for MCQ: 1. Nociceptive Pain: Clear anatomical proportionality between pain and mechanical stress; intermittent/sharp with movement, dull ache at rest; absence of dysesthesias; identifiable aggravating/easing mechanical factors. 2. Peripheral Neuropathic Pain: Pain in dermatomal or cutaneous nerve distribution; history of nerve injury or compression; sensory abnormalities (allodynia, hyperalgesia, numbness) mapped to nerve territory; positive neural provocation tests (SLR, Upper Limb Neurodynamic Tests). 3. Central Sensitization Pain: Disproportionate, non-mechanical, unpredictable pain pattern; widespread neuroanatomical distribution (non-dermatomal); diffuse tenderness to light palpation; high association with maladaptive psychosocial factors (catastrophizing, distress) and central symptoms (fatigue, sleep disturbance, hyperreactivity to sensory stimuli).
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Nociceptive = Clear mechanical/anatomical proportionality | Peripheral Neuropathic = Dermatomal/nerve distribution + neurodynamic findings | Central Sensitization = Disproportionate, widespread, non-mechanical + hyperreactivity.

📄 Finnerup et al. (2016) — Neuropathic Pain: Updated Grading System for Clinical & Research Practice

  • Citation: Pain, 157(8):1599–606. DOI: 10.1097/j.pain.0000000000000600.
  • Evidence Quality: IASP NeuPSIG International Consensus Classification.
  • Diagnostic Grading Steps (Essential for MCQ): - Step 1: Pain with a distinct neuroanatomically plausible distribution + History suggestive of a relevant neurological lesion or disease. - Possible Neuropathic Pain: Satisfies Step 1 (History + Neuroanatomical distribution). No clinical examination yet required. - Probable Neuropathic Pain: Satisfies Step 1 + Demonstration of distinct sensory signs (positive or negative sensory signs like hypoesthesia, allodynia, pinprick hyperalgesia) confined to the neuroanatomical distribution of the lesion during clinical examination. - Definite Neuropathic Pain: Satisfies Step 1 + Step 2 + Objective confirmatory diagnostic test proving the lesion/disease (e.g., MRI showing nerve root compression, biopsy showing small fiber neuropathy, EMG/nerve conduction studies, surgical confirmation).
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Possible = Plausible history + Anatomical distribution | Probable = Adds clinical sensory examination signs | Definite = Adds objective diagnostic confirmation (EMG/MRI/Biopsy).

📄 Simons et al. (2014) & Malfliet et al. (2017) — Neural Systems of Psychological Processing & Brain Changes

  • Citations: Neurosci Biobehav Rev, 39:61–78 & Eur J Pain, 21(5):769–86.
  • Evidence Quality: Systematic reviews of functional neuroimaging and structural morphometry.
  • Key Neural Circuitry for MCQ: - Fear-Avoidance Brain Circuit: Pain-related fear and perceived threat activate the Amygdala and Dorsal Anterior Cingulate Cortex (dACC), disrupting normal prefrontal inhibitory control. - Reward Deficiency Syndrome: Chronic pain impairs dopaminergic signaling in the Nucleus Accumbens (ventral striatum) and ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC), leading to anhedonia and loss of reward-driven active behavior. - Catastrophizing & Structural Morphometry (Malfliet 2017): High pain catastrophizing is directly associated with reduced gray matter volume in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) (impairing top-down pain gating) and hyperactivity in the default mode network (DMN) during rest.
  • ⚡ MCQ Memorization Formula:
    Threat/Fear = Amygdala + dACC hyperactivity | Chronic inactivity = Nucleus Accumbens reward signaling deficiency | High Catastrophizing = DLPFC gray matter thinning + impaired top-down descending inhibition.

10. Top 20 EFIC MCQ Distractor Traps & Rules

  1. The Acceptance & Mindfulness Trap (Veehof 2016): - Trap: "Acceptance- and mindfulness-based interventions demonstrate high-quality evidence for moderate reductions in pain intensity." - Reality: FALSE. Evidence quality is low-to-high. Effect on pain intensity is small ($\text{SMD} = 0.37$). Moderate effects occur ONLY on pain interference ($\text{SMD} = 0.62$) and anxiety ($\text{SMD} = 0.51$).
  2. The Ultrasound Trap (Ebadi 2014): - Trap: "Cochrane reviews recommend therapeutic ultrasound as an effective adjunctive treatment for chronic low back pain." - Reality: FALSE. Cochrane review found NO evidence of benefit over sham. Ultrasound is ineffective and not recommended.
  3. The SMT Superiority Trap (Rubinstein 2011): - Trap: "Spinal manipulative therapy (SMT) is clinically superior to exercise therapy for chronic low back pain." - Reality: FALSE. SMT produces small short-term improvements that are equivalent to exercise and standard medical care, NOT superior.
  4. The GMI Sequence Trap (Bowering 2013): - Trap: "In severe CRPS, mirror therapy should be instituted immediately to provide rapid visual cortical feedback." - Reality: FALSE. GMI must follow the strict sequential order: (1) Laterality recognition $\rightarrow$ (2) Imagined movements $\rightarrow$ (3) Mirror therapy. Disrupting the sequence worsens symptoms.
  5. The Multidisciplinary Durability Trap (Kamper 2015): - Trap: "Multidisciplinary biopsychosocial rehabilitation shows its greatest advantage over physical therapy in the first 2 to 4 weeks of treatment." - Reality: FALSE. Multidisciplinary rehab shows its primary advantage in long-term follow-up ($>12\text{ months}$), specifically for sustained return to work ($\text{OR} = 1.87$) and reduced sick leave.
  6. The Education Equivalence Trap (Engers 2008): - Trap: "Brief educational booklets (like The Back Book) are equally effective for acute and chronic low back pain." - Reality: FALSE. Brief advice is effective for acute LBP, but ineffective for chronic LBP (which requires intensive, structured biopsychosocial education).
  7. The Standalone PNE Trap (Moseley 2015, Geneen 2015): - Trap: "Pain Neuroscience Education delivered as a standalone lecture is sufficient to eliminate chronic pain-related disability." - Reality: FALSE. Standalone PNE improves knowledge and catastrophizing, but has minimal impact on disability unless paired with active movement and functional exposure.
  8. The Nocebo Mechanism Trap (Petersen 2014): - Trap: "Nocebo hyperalgesia is purely a psychological construct with no identifiable neurochemical basis." - Reality: FALSE. Nocebo hyperalgesia has a moderate-to-large effect size ($g = 0.63$), is biochemically mediated by the Cholecystokinin (CCK) system, and is blocked by proglumide.
  9. The Exercise Superiority Trap (Searle 2015, Geneen 2017): - Trap: "Specific core motor control exercise is proven to be substantially superior to all other exercise forms for long-term chronic back pain relief." - Reality: FALSE. Motor control and resistance exercises show slightly higher short-term effects, but long-term outcomes (12 months) are broadly equivalent across active exercise modalities.
  10. The Graded Activity vs. Exposure Trap (Macedo 2010): - Trap: "Graded Activity and Graded Exposure operate on the identical psychological mechanism." - Reality: FALSE. Graded Activity uses operant conditioning (quota-based pacing). Graded Exposure uses cognitive-behavioral in-vivo threat disconfirmation (fear hierarchies / PHODA).
  11. The Pediatric Solicitousness Trap (Eccleston 2015): - Trap: "Treating the child with chronic pain is sufficient; parental responses have no measurable impact on clinical outcomes." - Reality: FALSE. Parental solicitousness directly maintains child disability; parent-targeted psychological interventions significantly reduce parental distress ($\text{SMD} = -0.27$) and improve child school attendance.
  12. The TENS in Phantom Limb Trap (Johnson 2015): - Trap: "Cochrane reviews confirm high-quality proof that TENS completely resolves phantom limb pain." - Reality: FALSE. Cochrane reviews classify RCT evidence for TENS in phantom limb pain as inconclusive.
  13. The NSAID in Neuropathic Pain Trap: - Trap: "High-dose NSAIDs are recommended as first-line therapy for neuropathic pain and central sensitization." - Reality: FALSE. NSAIDs target peripheral prostaglandins; they have no efficacy in neuropathic pain or central sensitization and carry severe gastrointestinal and renal risks.
  14. The Dementia Pain Measurement Trap (Lichtner 2014, Herr 2011): - Trap: "In patients with severe Alzheimer's dementia, heart rate and blood pressure fluctuations are superior to behavioral observation tools." - Reality: FALSE. Autonomic signs are non-specific and habituate; validated observational scales (PAINAD, MOBID-2, Abbey) are gold standard when self-report fails.
  15. The Inpatient Arthroplasty Rehab Trap (Khan 2008): - Trap: "All post-total knee arthroplasty patients should undergo routine inpatient multidisciplinary rehabilitation for superior 1-year outcomes." - Reality: FALSE. Routine inpatient multidisciplinary rehabilitation does not outperform home-based exercise at 12 months.
  16. The Daily Activity Assumption Trap (Oliveira 2016): - Trap: "Patients who complete 3 physical therapy exercise sessions a week automatically increase their daily physical activity levels." - Reality: FALSE. Standard exercise therapy does not change habitual daily physical activity unless daily behavioral pacing and tracking are explicitly prescribed.
  17. The Dementia Gold Standard Trap (Lichtner 2014): - Trap: "The PAINAD scale has been confirmed as the definitive gold standard observational instrument across all dementia populations." - Reality: FALSE. Meta-review of 28 tools demonstrates that NO single tool can be recommended as a universal gold standard due to widespread psychometric variability.
  18. The Post-Surgical Risk Factor Trap (Wang 2016): - Trap: "Mastectomy carries a significantly higher risk of persistent post-surgical pain than breast-conserving lumpectomy, and older age is a risk factor." - Reality: FALSE. Surgical type (mastectomy vs. lumpectomy) and chemotherapy have no significant association with persistent pain. Younger age increases risk ($\text{OR} = 1.36$ per decade younger), and axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) is the strongest predictor ($\text{OR} = 2.41$).
  19. The Neurological Scale Selection Trap (Tyson 2013): - Trap: "Patients with right-hemisphere stroke lesions perform best on visual analog and pictorial scales." - Reality: FALSE. Right-hemisphere stroke patients frequently suffer from spatial neglect and visual distortion; they perform better on verbal rating scales (VRS). Patients with left-hemisphere stroke (aphasia) perform better on visual or numeric scales.

  20. The Possible Neuropathic Pain Trap (Finnerup 2016): - Trap: "Diagnosing 'Possible Neuropathic Pain' requires objective confirmatory tests such as EMG or nerve biopsy." - Reality: FALSE. 'Possible' requires only a plausible history and neuroanatomically compatible distribution. Clinical sensory signs make it 'Probable'. Confirmatory diagnostic tests are required for 'Definite'.

  21. The Electronic Activity Feedback Trap (Oliveira 2018): - Trap: "Providing chronic MSK pain patients with wearable activity trackers and smartphone feedback apps is an effective standalone intervention to reduce pain and disability." - Reality: FALSE. Meta-analysis (Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2018) shows electronic feedback alone is ineffective in reducing pain or disability without clinician-led behavioral pacing and counseling.

  22. The Exercise in Central Sensitization Trap (Lima 2017, Sluka 2018): - Trap: "Intense exercise always produces exercise-induced hypoalgesia regardless of the patient's pain mechanism." - Reality: FALSE. In patients with central sensitization (fibromyalgia, CWP), unaccustomed or vigorous eccentric exercise causes exercise-induced hyperalgesia (flare-up) due to deficient descending inhibition (impaired CPM) and heightened dorsal horn excitability. Prescription must start with low-intensity aerobic or submaximal isometrics ($20\text{–}25\%$ MVC).
  23. The Cancer Pain Recurrence Trap (De Groef 2019): - Trap: "Any persistent musculoskeletal or neuropathic pain after breast cancer treatment should be presumed to indicate cancer recurrence and exercise should be ceased." - Reality: FALSE. Persistent pain after cancer (PPAC) is common (~30–50%) due to surgical trauma, radiation fibrosis, aromatase inhibitors, and CIPN. Supervised exercise is safe and effective; PNE must explicitly reassure patients that non-tumoral altered neurobiology explains persistent symptoms.
  24. The Pediatric Return-to-School Trap (Harrison 2019): - Trap: "Children with chronic pain should remain absent from school until their pain score drops below 3/10." - Reality: FALSE. Best-evidence pediatric rehabilitation is function-first; school attendance is prioritized regardless of pain intensity via structured graded return-to-school (RTS) plans. Waiting for pain resolution promotes severe social disability and isolation.
  25. The Community Dementia Agitation Trap (Bullock 2019): - Trap: "Antipsychotic medications are first-line for new-onset agitation and behavioral disturbance in dementia patients." - Reality: FALSE. Agitation is frequently an atypical manifestation of untreated physical pain. A structured stepwise pain management protocol (STA-OP, starting with scheduled paracetamol) significantly reduces behavioral agitation without antipsychotic risks.
  26. The Contextual Factors Trap (Testa & Rossettini 2016): - Trap: "Placebo effects in physiotherapy are illusory noise that clinicians should ignore or attempt to eliminate." - Reality: FALSE. Contextual factors trigger real, measurable neurobiological analgesia ($g \approx 0.5\text{–}0.7$) mediated by endogenous opioids and endocannabinoids. Clinicians have an ethical responsibility to maximize positive contextual cues while avoiding deceit.
